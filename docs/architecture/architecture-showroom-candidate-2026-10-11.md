## Product decision

The reader wants to choose an AI application, understand its components, inspect its code, and open a real deployment setup. The live hub provided a field guide and official references; its separate template shop carried disabled coming-soon offers. This candidate adds a visual entrance and an interactive build explorer to that existing hub.

Keep the field guide, its pinned source, the official atlas, and all current URLs. Reuse `ArchitectureDiagram` and the already merged FrankX starters. Keep diagram previews separate from model execution and measured telemetry. Use the existing Higgsfield referral hop; do not invent hosting affiliate IDs.

Budget: one route, one shared-diagram extension, four source-backed previews, three original editorial images. Stop before merging or claiming production readiness if required checks, source links, independent review, or rendered evidence remain unresolved.

## Three directions compared

1. **Architecture museum:** cinematic physical artwork, large editorial type, then a precise build explorer. Selected: it gives the work a distinctive entrance while keeping technical diagrams inspectable.
2. **Workbench first:** full-viewport graph and dense controls. Strong for repeat builders; weaker for a first visit and mobile comprehension.
3. **Template storefront:** product gallery, deployment actions, and bundles first. Strong commercial routing; premature where fulfilment or checkout is unverified.

The selected direction combines museum imagery with the existing diagram component and quiet offer rows. The artwork is symbolic. Source diagrams and limits carry the technical claims.
