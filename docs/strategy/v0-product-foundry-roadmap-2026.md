# FrankX Product Foundry roadmap

Date: 2026-08-28

## Decision

Build the **FrankX Product Foundry**, not a v0 clone.

v0 remains an optional upstream design accelerator. FrankX owns the product
specification, reusable kernel, vertical modules, provenance, release evidence,
and deployment path. The long-term product is a compiler that turns an audience,
offer, workflow, and brand system into a reviewable repository—not a new
foundation model or a trademark-confusing copy of v0.

## Current truth

- /v0 is a product foundry and interface-study catalog.
- Creator Launch OS is the first isolated public template with an MIT license,
  clean repository, CI, and Vercel deployment path.
- The 19 v0 outputs are interface studies. They are not represented as
  deployable products without public source and release evidence.
- The embedded preview theater loads one study at a time and keeps the external
  origin sandboxed.
- This is not yet a prompt-to-production generator.

## Experience thesis

- **Recipient:** an independent creator or AI founder choosing how to launch a
  digital product.
- **Job:** find a close vertical starting point, inspect working behavior, and
  deploy or commission the next credible version.
- **Promise:** every maturity label maps to inspectable source and evidence.
- **Primary action:** deploy the free starter.
- **Signature proof:** an embedded interface study beside a public source,
  release gates, and a clean-room provenance policy.
- **Feeling:** quiet technical confidence; no marketplace theater.

## Three directions reviewed

1. **Gallery first.** Nineteen large preview cards, device controls, and many
   equal-weight actions. Strong browsing, weak product truth, high visual and
   runtime weight.
2. **Blueprint graph.** A deep intelligence map connecting studies, systems,
   and repositories. Strong internal research value, too much taxonomy for a
   first-time visitor.
3. **Product foundry.** One free flagship, one governed embedded preview, clear
   maturity labels, a compact study index, and a staged portfolio. Selected
   because it makes the next action and the evidence legible.

## Owned technical moat

The moat is the governed path from intent to a reliable product:

1. **Product brief schema** — audience, buying moment, offer, decisive workflow,
   required proof, brand pack, data sensitivity, and business model.
2. **Template registry** — source, license, provenance, maturity, demo, deploy
   path, environment variables, security posture, and verification receipts.
3. **Shared kernel** — identity, content, catalog, pricing, checkout adapter,
   entitlements, delivery, email, analytics, support, privacy, observability,
   and deployment configuration.
4. **Vertical modules** — audience-specific pages, workflows, states, vocabulary,
   and proof.
5. **Product compiler** — selects the kernel and vertical modules, applies brand
   tokens and content, creates a repository, and opens a preview deployment.
6. **Evaluation loop** — type, lint, contract, build, accessibility, responsive
   capture, links, claims, privacy, analytics, performance, and rollback.
7. **Provider adapters** — Vercel for deployment; optional Polar, Stripe, or
   LemonSqueezy commerce adapters; provider-neutral core.

## Release taxonomy

- **Study:** interactive reference; no source or deploy claim.
- **Prototype:** source may exist; security and clean deployment are incomplete.
- **Starter:** documented source, license, setup, core states, and CI.
- **Deployable:** clean-account deployment plus environment and rollback proof.
- **Commercial kit:** deployable plus maintained integrations, fulfillment,
  updates, support, and an explicit support window.

## Commerce and Polar

Keep every free template useful without payment credentials. Add commerce through
one server-side adapter contract:

- create checkout;
- verify webhook signature;
- map product and price to entitlement;
- record fulfillment idempotently;
- expose customer access without leaking provider secrets.

Polar is a strong first optional adapter for software and digital-product
businesses because checkout, tax handling, webhooks, and entitlements can remain
behind that contract. It should not be hardwired into the public kernel. A
template must still render an honest “checkout not configured” state.

## Source and rights policy

- Aggregate only official, public, or explicitly licensed material.
- Record source URL, author, license, retrieval date, and allowed use.
- Link or embed public v0 studies as references; do not imply ownership.
- Do not copy paid source, prompts, assets, or private template code.
- For commercial products, write a clean-room behavior specification from
  observable workflows, then implement original code and design.
- Reject a release when rights or attribution cannot be established.

## Next engineering milestones

1. Ship /v0 with the embedded preview, sentence-case interface, analytics,
   and production receipt.
2. Upgrade Creator Launch OS from release candidate after desktop, mobile,
   keyboard, reduced-motion, link, and performance evidence passes.
3. Define product-foundry.manifest.json and validate it in CI.
4. Extract the shared kernel into versioned packages without turning the public
   starters into a monorepo dependency trap.
5. Build Author & Book Launch OS and AI SaaS Starter as the two contrasting
   proofs of the compiler.
6. Add a prompt-to-brief interface only after the manifest and evaluation
   contracts are stable.
7. Add repository creation and Vercel preview orchestration with explicit human
   approval before production promotion.

## Success measures

- visitor reaches a source, preview, or deploy action;
- clean-account deploy succeeds;
- first useful edit takes less than fifteen minutes;
- no unsupported maturity, licensing, or performance claims;
- percentage of shared-kernel changes adopted across maintained templates;
- activation, successful delivery, and second-use events by vertical.
