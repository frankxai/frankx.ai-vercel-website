# Community Platform Guide — Direction Decision

**Decision date:** 27 July 2026
**Approver:** Socrates — independent visual-direction approver
**Verifier:** Gibbs — independent engineering verifier
**Maker:** Codex — implementation and release lead
**Status:** Direction 1 selected; Directions 2 and 3 rejected for this release. Production approval remains evidence-gated.

## Direction 1 — The Research Atlas

An authored architecture plate that behaves like a responsive research instrument. The verified platform registry is the visual identity. The page uses an asymmetric decision topology, a platform constellation, a control/liability rail, an AI permission topology, and a 90-day evidence line.

Three-second read: “This is researched, current, and willing to choose. Circle is the phase-one answer; ownership expands only when evidence earns it.”

Production risk: moderate.
FrankX fit: strongest.
Decision: **approved**.

## Direction 2 — The Decision Ledger

A founder field manual built from typographic spreads, source margins, documented product fragments, and evidence/consequence annotations. It maximizes due-diligence authority and print quality.

Three-second read: “This is the decision memo to read before committing a year, a brand, and an audience.”

Production risk: low to moderate.
FrankX fit: strong, but more archival and less alive than the selected direction.
Decision: rejected for the primary web experience; viable later as a downloadable companion.

## Direction 3 — The Community Observatory

A full-viewport decision engine. Brand control, member behavior, and operating-responsibility inputs recompute the recommended platform and redraw the dependency topology.

Three-second read: “This is a decision product, not a platform list.”

Production risk: highest.
FrankX fit: strong as a later tool, but excessive before the registry and recommendation logic are proven in production.
Decision: rejected for this release.

## Approval Rationale

The Research Atlas wins because it turns evidence into the visual system, preserves FrankX’s technical mode without repeating its card grammar, reflows deliberately on mobile, and gives motion one explanatory job: showing the approval boundary between AI read operations and consequential writes.

## Selected Composition System

1. Hero: the ownership decision.
2. Platform constellation: choose by behavior, then control.
3. Build/own spectrum: control acquired, liability inherited.
4. AI topology: read, reason, approve, act.
5. 90-day system: evidence accumulates, architecture follows.

## Constraints

- No synthetic creator likeness.
- No fake product interface.
- No rainbow platform palette.
- No wall of equal cards.
- No rasterized article text.
- No universal vendor quality score.
- Platform capabilities must come from one dated registry.
- The static state must remain complete without animation.
- Mobile must recompose, not scale down.
