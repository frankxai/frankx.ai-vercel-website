# Community Platform Guide — Experience Thesis

## Recipient

An established creator with meaningful distribution, high taste, and a serious community or learning-product ambition. She is choosing between a hosted platform, a branded app, a headless layer, and a custom product. She does not need another affiliate roundup; she needs a defensible architecture decision.

## Job To Be Done

Choose what to rent, what to control, and what to build before a platform decision turns into operational debt.

## Promise

In one sitting, the reader can:

- identify the member behavior that must be proprietary;
- compare current community products by ownership layer and AI-connectivity path;
- distinguish native MCP from API-, webhook-, and automation-based agent access;
- choose a credible 90-day launch lane;
- know the evidence threshold for a branded app, headless build, or fully custom product.

## Signature Proof

A current, source-led platform registry that separates:

- verified capability from vendor claim;
- native MCP from “an MCP can be built”;
- shared mobile access from a dedicated App Store listing;
- white-label presentation from interface and product-model ownership;
- established products from emerging platforms that still require diligence.

## Desired Feel

An editorial architecture observatory: calm, exact, inspectable, and more like a senior product decision room than a software-review blog.

## Primary Action

Use the decision model to select a validation lane and download the underlying platform registry.

## Secondary Action

Read the 90-day proof plan and define the one repeated member behavior that the platform must support.

## Experience Principles

1. Decision before directory.
2. Provenance next to claims.
3. One dominant recommendation, with explicit exceptions.
4. Dense evidence without dashboard cosplay.
5. Mobile is a first-class reading and decision surface.
6. Motion only when it explains a change of ownership or architecture.
7. No score that pretends unlike platforms are universally comparable.

## Release Non-Negotiables

- Exactly 3 materially different art directions documented before implementation.
- The selected direction has an explicit approver and rationale.
- Current desktop and mobile FrankX captures preserved as the baseline.
- Official sources for decisive product claims.
- A dated verification field and a clear “watchlist” status for immature entrants.
- Keyboard-accessible filters and tables.
- Reduced-motion behavior.
- No horizontal page overflow at 375 px.
- Production commit, rollback reference, and post-deploy route checks.
