## 1 · Why the pilots fail contemporary editorial taste

**a) The scale mistake is the whole problem.** The official master (`frank-omega-pixar-blue-v1`) works because there is *no real-world scale reference* in the frame — he's a graphic object on a controlled field, so he reads as a confident digital twin with a clean silhouette. Every pilot destroys that by putting a photoreal human, a guitar, or a suited man beside him. The moment a real adult sets the scale, FRANK-Ω becomes a **15-centimeter desk toy**. He is demoted from *twin* to *figurine* in all four frames. That is decoration, exactly what the charter forbids.

**b) Two materialities that can't share a frame.** Each image welds photographic capture (skin, wood grain, fabric, a real hand) to a smooth subsurface-scattered 3D cartoon. The eye can't decide whether it's looking at a photograph or a render, flags the composite, and files it under "AI feed." Serious contemporary editorial commits to *one* register — photograph, illustration, or information design. It never pastes a toy render into a stock photo.

**c) The scene is the most saturated trope of the era.** "A human does a craft while a cute robot supervises" is a visual cliché — the image equivalent of a stock phrase. Nothing in any frame is specific to *ACOS* versus any generic "AI helps creators" post. The content promise is invisible, precisely as the charter diagnoses.

**d) Borrowed cinema, no point of view.** Gooseneck lamp, dusk window, rim-lit bokeh, shallow depth of field — this is the default "moody creative at night" lighting kit. Competent and completely anonymous. `taste.md` is explicit: *"Cinema, not stock. Compose, don't generate-and-pray."* These are generate-and-pray.

**e) The dead-left field is a reflex, not a composition.** All four park the subject right and reserve a soft-focus left third for a title. That violates Design Law 3 head-on: *"Build image and deterministic type as one composition, not image plus a safe zone."*

**f) Cuteness fights the brand.** North star: *Elite. Architect. Humble.* A hands-on-hips chibi with oversized anime eyes reads as a consumer-gadget mascot. On the **enterprise/production** article it actively undercuts the seriousness the copy is working to earn.

The pilots are a skilled answer to the wrong brief. Kill them and start from the idea, not the scene.

## 2 · Three territories — genuinely different at the level of composition and story

Each maps to exactly one surface archetype (per the design laws), which guarantees they diverge on all four axes — not on color.

| Axis | **T1 · The Console** | **T2 · The Four Faders** | **T3 · One Person, Studio Scale** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Archetype | Studio artifact | Technical proof plate | Editorial cover |
| Composition | Top-down flat-lay, full-bleed specimen tray | Centered severe schematic, ink-on-obsidian | Enormous type interlocked with a field of evidence |
| Image ↔ type | Title *engraved into* the panel as a label, same plane | Headline is the plate's top register; labels share its type system | Type is the loudest graphic; the image is the ground |
| Story | Fragmented tools become one operable instrument | A serious system chooses constraints before it automates | One human, systems that compound, studio-scale output |
| Materiality | Real paper, ink, anodized metal, matte | Ink on obsidian, hairline rules, one signal color | Print-grade, tactile, no lifestyle scene |

None uses a photoreal environment with a composited toy. That is the point.

## 3 · Concept cards

### T1 — **THE CONSOLE**
- **Article:** *The Complete Guide to Agentic Creator OS*
- **Image thesis (one line):** *Twelve scattered tools resolve into a single labeled control surface you could actually put your hands on.*
- **Type coexistence, from the first sketch:** the title is set as the console's engraved header — Poppins display for the name, JetBrains Mono for the card labels — printed *on* the surface, occupying the top register of the grid. No floating title, no margin.
- **Must be absent:** any human figure; the chibi robot as a character; screen/dashboard captures or fake UI; holograms; glow used as decoration; the empty left third; lifestyle desk clutter.
- **FRANK-Ω:** **No, not as a figure.** He may exist only as a printed maker's-mark — a small index card or stamp *within* the artifact. A credit, not a character. He does not stand in the frame.
- **Call:** **RENDER.** This is the strongest of the three. It proves the article's exact promise ("fragmented tools become a personal creative OS that compounds") and is the least reproducible by an AI feed.

### T2 — **THE FOUR FADERS**
- **Article:** *Enterprise Agentic Architecture: Decision Framework for Production*
- **Image thesis:** *Before any automation, four faders set the constraints — the decision itself is the hero, drawn as a precise instrument, never a UI.*
- **Type coexistence:** the headline forms the plate's top rule; the four decision words and their scale marks are set in the same type system as the diagram — one composition, deterministic text composited, centered on near-black.
- **Must be absent:** server rooms; datacenters; any robot; dashboards or fake interfaces; 3D holographic charts; a suited stock "businessman"; neon.
- **FRANK-Ω:** **No — absolute.** The article's posture is seriousness → clarity. A character contradicts the plate's authority.
- **Call:** **HOLD.** The concept is right and ownable (it comes straight from the article's own controlling metaphor, which keeps it off the server-room cliché). The execution risk is real: it must be resolved to a **restrained schematic**, not literal skeuomorphic studio-gear photography and not a generic infographic. Resolve that fork before a source master is rendered.

### T3 — **ONE PERSON, STUDIO SCALE**
- **Article:** *The Golden Age of Creators: Why Now Is Different*
- **Image thesis:** *One origin point expands into a studio's worth of output — the leverage of a single human made visible, with the cost inversion as the headline.*
- **Type coexistence:** cover-grade. One figure set enormous — `12,000` or `$90` — interlocks with a composed field of small marks; the masthead headline crops into that field. The type carries the frame; the field is the ground it sits on.
- **Must be absent:** person-at-laptop; aspirational sunrise; any robot; a crowd; an "AI brain"; a generic data-viz dashboard.
- **FRANK-Ω:** **No.** The essay's subject is the *human* creator's leverage. A robot hero relocates authorship to the machine and triggers the "AI utopia" shortcut the brief explicitly bans.
- **Call:** **RENDER** — with the discipline that the field of marks must read as *authored evidence*, not stock data-viz.

## 4 · Where FRANK-Ω actually earns his place

He is absent from all three heroes on purpose. That is the honest editorial read: **the strongest, most contemporary FrankX directions do not use him as a scene character.** The brief already licenses this — *"a stronger concept without him wins over a weaker concept with him."*

If he ships anywhere in this reboot, it is only in his **native register**: a self-contained digital-twin *portrait* on a controlled obsidian field, cover typography locked to his silhouette, at full graphic scale — the master's own language — used as a brand or product-face asset, **never composited into a photoreal scene at toy scale.** And even on ACOS, where he is literally the product's face, **The Console out-argues the portrait.** So: hold him out of the three article heroes, and reserve the portrait for masthead/brand use where his scale is protected.
