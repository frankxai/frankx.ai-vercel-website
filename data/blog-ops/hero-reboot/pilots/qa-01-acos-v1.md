# Independent QA — Pilot 01: ACOS Studio That Remembers

**Verdict:** REJECT — do not compose or integrate this source frame.

## What worked

- A real person, guitar, notebook, audio gear, and late-night lamp create a more human and topic-relevant atmosphere than the retired object-metaphor batch.
- The left/right contrast makes the "fragmented work → organised practice" premise legible at a glance.
- The creator is working rather than staring at camera; the frame has an observable human consequence.

## Why it fails

1. **Generated readable text violates the production rule.** Multiple handwritten pages, the wall document, and a visible emergency-exit sign contain model-rendered marks/text. Exact words must be deterministic, and source frames cannot introduce fake typographic noise.
2. **The split is too literal.** The wide pale diagonal divider reads as an art-director device pasted over a generic messy-desk photograph, not an earned visual narrative.
3. **The "before" side is clutter, not a specific problem.** It says "overwhelmed creative" rather than prompt amnesia/tool fragmentation. The distinction needs to come from composition and the final title, not piles of illegible paper.
4. **The image has no controlled social type zone.** A title overlay would fight the face, lamp, notebook, and paper everywhere.
5. **Craft is below premium source-frame bar.** Wallpaper, exposed cables, emergency signage, and paper clutter make the shot feel like a generated documentary still rather than a designed FrankX editorial image.

## Smallest viable revision

Keep the human, instrument, late-night work, and meaningful contrast. Remove all pages/signage/screens with visible marks; use unmarked tactile materials and a subtler spatial transition. Reserve a quiet but intentional region for deterministic social typography without returning to empty-background safe-zone logic.
