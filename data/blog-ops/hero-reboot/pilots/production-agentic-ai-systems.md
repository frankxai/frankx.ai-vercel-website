# Pilot brief — Enterprise Agentic Architecture: Decision Framework for Production

## Editorial truth

- **Primary reader:** technical leader or architect deciding whether an agentic initiative deserves production investment.
- **Article promise:** choose the appropriate maturity level and architecture instead of treating a convincing demo as a deployable product.
- **Share hook:** "The useful question is not how to build agents—it is whether this needs agents at all, and what level of reliability is justified."
- **Evidence available in article:** the demo-to-production gap, four decision factors, four maturity tiers, six planes, explicit trade-offs, quality gates, costs, and human checkpoints.
- **Image-worthy sentence:** *At 2 AM, a prototype can look perfect; the real work is deciding what must still be true when it meets customers, budgets, and failure.*
- **Territory:** Evidence in Context.
- **Desired response:** intellectual confidence and relief from hype.

## Direction A — Conservative: The decision table, made editorial

**Composition:** A dramatic technical editorial cover built around an accurate deterministic four-axis decision board: uncertainty, consequence, autonomy, and operating cost. A subtle high-end source frame sits behind it—a real systems architect's working surface at night—but all important labels and diagrams are composed deterministically.

**Why it earns attention:** It shows the article's actual utility, not a metaphor for AI. At social size, the reader sees a decision framework; in the blog hero, the diagram becomes a trustworthy entry point.

**Risk to avoid:** Fake dashboards, unreadable tiny charts, or corporate-control-room cliché.

## Direction B — Strong fit: The moment a demo becomes a responsibility

**Composition:** A cinematic, human-scale systems review. One technical leader stands at a large wall of accurate architecture artefacts and test evidence. The composition reveals a clear boundary: a bright, seductive demo area on one side and a calmer, disciplined production architecture with quality gates and human checkpoints on the other. The real diagram and exact language are added in post-production.

**Why it earns attention:** The image has stakes. It makes the article's core distinction emotionally and technically legible: working once is not the same as being safe, governable, observable, and worth paying for.

**Deterministic social composition:**
- category: `AI ARCHITECTURE`
- title: `A demo is not a production system.`
- subline: `The decision framework for agentic AI`

**Risk to avoid:** A superhero engineer, a giant holographic screen, or a fictional enterprise UI.

## Direction C — Divergent: The architecture as a stage plan

**Composition:** An original theatre-stage analogy drawn from the article itself. An empty, impeccably prepared stage is seen from the wings: rigging, marked zones, and a visible safety/production plan. The deterministic overlay maps the six planes and maturity decision without pretending that a stage *is* an agent system.

**Why it earns attention:** It honours the article's music/stage language while making the governance point visually memorable.

**Risk to avoid:** Turning the article into whimsical theatre art instead of a serious decision framework.

## Recommendation

**Advance Direction B with Direction A's deterministic information layer.** The hero needs human stakes; the share card needs a real framework. Combining them gives both relevance and proof.

## Required derivatives
