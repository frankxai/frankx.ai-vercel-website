# Pilot brief — The Complete Guide to Agentic Creator OS v6

## Editorial truth

- **Primary reader:** a capable independent creator who is exhausted by tool roulette, lost prompts, and generic output.
- **Article promise:** turn Claude Code from a one-off chat into a persistent creative operating system with skills, agents, workflows, and quality gates.
- **Share hook:** "This explains the difference between using AI occasionally and building a creative system that compounds."
- **Evidence available in article:** 25 commands, 80+ skills, 40+ agents, 8 workflows, 12,000+ AI songs, a real website, and persistent creative practice.
- **Image-worthy sentence:** *A creator stops re-opening empty chats and begins working inside a studio that remembers how their work is made.*
- **Territory:** Studio Documentary.
- **Desired response:** relief followed by ambition.

## Direction A — Conservative: The studio that remembers

**Composition:** An intimate, late-night hybrid music-and-software studio from just behind a creator's shoulder. We see deliberate traces of their practice: a score marked by hand, a microphone, an audio interface, a command palette-like light source, and a wall of working references. The person is active but not posing; their attention is on making a decision.

**Why it earns attention:** It translates "persistent skills and context" into an emotionally legible world—your work continues where you left it. It feels like Frank's actual musician-technologist identity rather than an anonymous SaaS ad.

**Risk to avoid:** A generic neon desk, a back-of-head laptop cliché, fake terminal text, or a pile of unrelated props.

## Direction B — Strong fit: From prompt fragments to a working studio

**Composition:** A purposeful editorial diptych. Left: an unstable, tactile scatter of abandoned prompt fragments, half-finished ideas, and disconnected tools in soft shadow. Right: the same creative energy has become a composed, active studio session around a person at work. A controlled line of continuity crosses the seam—not a glowing "AI wire," but a tangible editorial device representing retained judgment.

**Why it earns attention:** The article's actual before/after is visible in one glance: not chat versus chat, but fragmented effort versus a system that remembers. The contrast supports a title-led social card and remains clear in an index thumbnail.

**Deterministic social composition:**
- category: `CREATOR SYSTEMS`
- title: `Your AI should remember how you work.`
- subline: `The complete guide to Agentic Creator OS`

**Risk to avoid:** Splitting the frame evenly, presenting fake UI, or making the "before" side look like visual clutter without a real narrative.

## Direction C — Divergent: The creator's operating score

**Composition:** A top-down art-directed scene with a creator's hand conducting a real working score: visible but unlabelled musical notation rhythm, cue cards, a compact system map, and physical tools arranged in one flowing composition. Exact technical labels and the title are added only through deterministic post-production.

**Why it earns attention:** It ties a real FrankX differentiator—musician-technologist practice—to the article's operational point. It is more authored and memorable than a generic computer scene.

**Risk to avoid:** Magical symbolism, fake music notation, or making a beautiful flat lay with no human decision.

## Recommendation

**Advance Direction B.** It carries the reader's problem, the promise, and the system change without requiring a generic AI motif. Direction C can become a secondary carousel/essay visual if the music connection is central in the final edit.

## Required derivatives

- Web 16:9: scene-first version, live H1 remains semantic in page layout.
- OG 1200×630: title-led Direction B composition.
- 4:5: one half of the narrative must survive without an arbitrary centre crop; re-compose vertically.
- 9:16: a single active studio moment plus deterministic title.

## QA questions

1. Does a creator recognize their actual problem before seeing the title?
2. Does the scene feel lived-in, specific, and human—not a generic tech ad?
3. Can a reviewer explain the change from fragmented effort to compounding system in one sentence?
