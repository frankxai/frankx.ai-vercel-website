# Mini Me Storyboard

Produce a coherent miniature world without flattening the person into a generic mascot. Work from approved identity photos; treat prior generated art as style or composition reference unless the user explicitly approves it as the canonical likeness.

## Workflow

1. **Audit references.** Inspect every source photo. Record stable identity traits and explicit corrections, then name one identity authority and one style authority. Note consent for other visible people. When editing a photo, preserve the requested untouched regions exactly.
2. **Lock a character bible.** Define face/hair/beard proportions, three-quarter silhouette, wardrobe variants, expressions, glasses policy, palette, scale, and explicit negative constraints. Generate a simple turnaround before a large batch when the tools support it. See [character-and-scene.md](references/character-and-scene.md).
3. **Storyboard first.** Give each card a different action, composition, material, setting, and emotional beat. One card should carry one idea. Include an opening hook, a change, a practical insight, and a human closing image. Specify aspect ratio and safe zones before generating.
4. **Generate one image per scene.** Use image generation for the character and environment. Include only references that serve the scene; reapply the identity bible every time. Match perspective, physical contact, shadow, reflected color, and depth. Keep all outputs separately reviewable.
5. **Build exact information separately.** For infographics, ask the image model for the art plate with reserved text areas. Set words, arrows, source labels, and diagram topology in deterministic SVG/HTML/Figma or another precise layout tool. Test the final at phone size.
6. **Review and revise.** Compare each image against the source face and other accepted cards. Correct the top few defects with region-specific instructions. Score likeness, action, crop, text, brand provenance, and emotional clarity. Do not report a consistent character from one lucky frame alone.
7. **Package the run.** Keep the exact prompt, source-reference map, generation result, caption, alt text, provenance, review notes, and export dimensions together. A reconstructed process timeline must be labeled as such; do not present it as a chat screenshot or measured benchmark.

## Character and brand boundaries

Distinguish an official mascot, an artistic rendering of one, a logo-inspired metaphor, and an original companion. Verify current first-party brand material before claiming official status or abilities. A pictured agent character does not prove a working integration or actual orchestration. Get necessary approval for promotional uses of third-party mascots; if unavailable, use a clearly labeled original character.

For Frank-specific work, his uploaded photos are the face authority. Preserve his swept/wavy brown hair with natural loose fringe, lighter beard coverage at the sides than at moustache/chin, and non-oversized head. Wardrobe can include a black shirt with black buttons or a black suit. If eyewear is shown, use dark sunglasses only. He should read as a curious adventurer and elegant director; other people are collaborators, not generic background extras. Do not impose these traits on another user's avatar.
