# Mini Me Storyboard: a repeatable visual-world workflow

**FrankX field guide · 29 September 2026**

Build a small cast that can enter your actual life, carry a story, and teach a useful idea. This pack reconstructs a real 20-image Mini Frank commission from its saved prompt and result collections. It is a creative case study, not a controlled model benchmark. The first collection made ten 4:5 editorial posts; the second made ten vertical scenes and infographics. The source conversation also included earlier iterations and changing direction, but this document does not claim to be a full chat transcript or a browser screenshot.

The [public Mini Me guide](/guides/mini-me-storyboard) shows the workflow and selected result previews. This companion file preserves selected verbatim user direction and all 20 exact generation prompts. The full private result archives remain with the creator.

## What changed through the work

| Pass | Direction and correction | What we kept |
|---|---|---|
| Personal world | Tiny 3D versions interact with real scenes, with contact shadows and scale. The original photo was requested to stay untouched. | Physical interaction before decorative particles. |
| Narrative system | The scenes began to teach multi-agent roles and show original or brand-derived companions. | Each post earns one specific idea and one emotional beat. |
| Character refinement | Frank asked for less literal realism mixed with expressive earlier styling, a more accurate swept/wavy hair silhouette, slimmer cheeks, lighter beard coverage on the sides, black shirts with black buttons or a black suit, and only dark sunglasses when eyewear appears. | One approved character reference controls identity; no generic oversized-head mascot. |
| Vertical series | Ten 9:16 compositions varied the storytelling grammar: cinematic cover, music sculpture, comic, paper architecture, rainy archive, gym scene, sea exploration, infographics, and ensemble. | Vary camera, material, action, and emotional temperature; do not repeat one layout ten times. |
| Review and export | Dense wording in generated images proved fragile. The final infographic plates used deterministic SVG for exact labels, with title and footer inside a mobile safe area. | Image generation for art; deterministic type and diagrams for facts. |

The saved prompt packages prove which prompts were used for the two finished collections. They do **not** prove a uniform likeness score, a live agent orchestration, or that a mascot has software capabilities. Review every image by eye before publishing.

## Start here: six prompts to copy

### 1. Reference audit and character bible

```text
I am making a recurring illustrated version of a real person. Examine the attached source photos as identity references. Describe stable traits, distinguishing details, hair silhouette, eye shape, beard density and side coverage, and clothing. Separate identity from artistic style. Make a compact character bible with an approved default outfit, two alternate outfits, three expressions, and an explicit list of things not to change. Do not infer unsupported biographical details. Ask for confirmation only if two source photos truly conflict. Keep other people in the photos out of the reusable identity model.
```

For Frank: use the photos, not a prior AI rendering, as the identity authority. Brown wavy/swept hair may have a loose fringe; beard is stronger at moustache/chin and lighter on the sides. Wardrobe may be black shirt with black buttons, black suit, or situational casual wear. Eyewear, when used, means dark sunglasses only.

### 2. Canonical character sheet

```text
Create one clean character sheet for [PERSON] using the attached approved identity photos and the character bible. Show front, three-quarter, and side views, plus two full-body poses. The style is refined stylized 3D illustration: recognizable human proportions, expressive face, tactile material, cinematic softness, and no plastic toy sheen. Preserve [HAIR], [FACIAL HAIR], [EYES], [DISTINCTIVE FEATURES]. Show [DEFAULT OUTFIT] and [ALTERNATE OUTFIT]. Neutral studio background and coherent scale. No invented logos, text, or extra characters. Treat this sheet as the visual continuity reference for subsequent scenes.
```

### 3. One scene with a story beat

```text
Create ONE finished [4:5 or 9:16] vertical editorial scene, not a contact sheet. Use the approved character sheet for identity and the attached real environment photograph for composition. Keep the photograph's architecture and people intact when the brief calls for photo editing; add the illustrated miniature as a separate, physically plausible layer. Mini [PERSON] is [ACTION] on [SPECIFIC SURFACE], with [SECOND CHARACTER] doing [COUNTER-ACTION]. Give them a clear relationship and one narrative turning point: [SETUP -> CHANGE]. Match light direction, perspective, scale, cast and contact shadows, reflected color, and depth of field. One clean focal point, room for a short headline if needed, no random interface, no illegible generated paragraphs. [EXACTLY WHICH PARTS OF PHOTO MUST REMAIN UNCHANGED].
```

### 4. Ten-card storyboard before generation

```text
Plan ten distinct vertical posts for [AUDIENCE] about [THEME]. For each, write: title (six words max), one-sentence insight, human emotion, real-world setting, miniature physical action, supporting character and its verified role, camera and lighting, aspect ratio, single proof or takeaway, and an alternate accessible caption. Use at least six different visual grammars (e.g. cinematic portrait, comic, still life, architectural cutaway, diagram, environmental scene). Make the story build toward a human invitation, not just a product parade. Flag every brand-owned character and every original invention. Do not generate images yet.
```

### 5. Exact infographic art plate

```text
Make the background illustration and character interaction for a [9:16] infographic about [ONE PROCESS]. Reserve a clear area for the heading and three short labels; do not render factual labels into the image. Show each stage through a distinct physical action and consistent character design. Return a composition with visual hierarchy and generous contrast. Separately provide exact copy, reading order, alt text, and source notes. Put the final typography and arrows in SVG, HTML/CSS, Figma, or a layout tool so spelling, order, and legibility can be checked. Keep critical text inside the central mobile-safe area.
```

### 6. Revision and release review

```text
Review the proposed final at phone size. Score 1-5 for likeness, silhouette, readable action, physical contact/shadow, compositional variety, factual accuracy, brand provenance, exact text, crop safety, and emotional clarity. State the top three defects with precise regions. Revise only those defects, preserving the approved identity, scene intent, and successful composition. Export the final image plus its prompt, references, version, caption, alt text, rights/provenance note, and a brief change log. Do not claim a character is an official mascot unless a current first-party source confirms it.
```
