# Higgsfield shot brief

Free FrankX production worksheet. Guide: https://www.frankx.ai/guides/higgsfield-ai-video-guide

The exercises below show a three-shot studio sequence: one person, one room, one physical action per shot. They are original exercise briefs, not benchmarks and not recorded generations. Credit cost, model, and job id stay blank until your account shows them. Copy the blank sheet at the bottom for your own shot.

## Example: shot 01, arrival

- Project and audience: First studio sequence for a creator. One person, one room, one physical action per shot. Target edit: 12 to 18 seconds across the three shots. Check the selected model for the clip lengths it supports.
- Viewer takeaway: The creator arrives in the studio workspace.
- Destination and aspect ratio: Review the cut first. Export for the destination you will actually publish, in that destination's aspect ratio.
- Shot ID and intended edit length: 01. Wide view, held steady for an establishing cut.
- Subject and setting: Creator walking through the studio entrance into the room with desk and piano. Daylit room, clean background.
- Authorized reference file: Approved character and room reference still. If room architecture, clothing, or hair is wrong in the still, do not animate it.
- Character / wardrobe / product details that must stay fixed: Wardrobe, hair color/style, door frame, window light, and room geometry.
- Subject movement: Person enters through the door, takes two steps into the room, and pauses looking toward the desk.
- One camera movement: Mostly static wide shot, subtle level drift only. No fast pan, no tilt, no cut.
- Lighting and visual style: Natural daylight from the left window. Consistent color temperature.
- Prompt version: v1, the arrival brief below.
- Model and settings actually selected:
- Acceptance rule (visible, checkable): Door frame and room geometry stay stable; subject likeness and wardrobe match the reference still; lighting direction remains fixed.
- Rejection rule: Warping door or walls, extra people appearing, changed wardrobe, sudden camera jerk, or identity morph.
- Credit cost shown before generation:
- Maximum attempts and credit ceiling: Write both before the first render.
- Job ID:
- First / middle / last frame review: Check room architecture at start, person's stride at middle, and stable pause at last frame.
- Accepted file and edit notes: Download accepted clip and trim head/tail to the usable stride and pause.

```text
Reference: the approved studio character and room.
Start: wide establishing view of studio room, entrance door visible.
Action: character enters through the door, takes two calm steps inside, and pauses facing the desk.
Camera: static wide angle, eye level, locked horizon, no pan or cut.
End: clean hold on the standing pause before any secondary motion.
Continuity: stable door and wall geometry, consistent natural daylight from left window.
Avoid: morphing architecture, extra figures, changing clothes, camera orbit, text.
```

## Example: shot 02, preparation

- Project and audience: First studio sequence for a creator. One person, one room, one physical action per shot.
- Viewer takeaway: The creator prepares their materials at the desk.
- Destination and aspect ratio: Same aspect ratio as shot 01.
- Shot ID and intended edit length: 02. Medium side view, focused on deliberate physical preparation.
- Subject and setting: Creator seated at the desk beside the piano, placing a closed notebook on the table surface.
- Authorized reference file: Approved reference still showing subject seated with notebook in hand.
- Character / wardrobe / product details that must stay fixed: Exact clothing, wristwatch/accessories, notebook color and size, desk position relative to window light.
- Subject movement: Creator places the closed notebook firmly on the desk surface and withdraws hand smoothly to rest.
- One camera movement: Medium side view, locked camera or very slow push-in. No rotation.
- Lighting and visual style: Same natural left-side window light as shot 01.
- Prompt version: v1, the preparation brief below.
- Model and settings actually selected:
- Acceptance rule (visible, checkable): Hand and notebook contact make anatomical sense; fingers do not fuse into notebook cover; clothing and hair match shot 01.
- Rejection rule: Deformed fingers or hands, notebook morphing or floating, table geometry shifting, wardrobe color change.
- Credit cost shown before generation:
- Maximum attempts and credit ceiling: Write both before the first render.
- Job ID:
- First / middle / last frame review: Check hand structure at contact, notebook edges during placement, and natural hand release at final frame.
- Accepted file and edit notes: Trim right after the notebook settles and hand rests.

```text
Reference: the approved studio character and room.
Start: medium side profile of character seated at the wooden desk.
Action: character deliberately places a closed notebook onto the desk surface and lets hands rest.
Camera: locked medium shot, eye level, stable framing, no rotation.
End: hand rests naturally beside the stationary notebook.
Continuity: anatomically correct hand and fingers, same clothing, same window light as shot 01.
Avoid: fused fingers, melting props, shifting desk edges, camera pans, scene cuts.
```

## Example: shot 03, the decision

- Project and audience: First studio sequence for a creator. One person, one room, one physical action per shot. Target edit: 12 to 18 seconds across the three shots. Check the selected model for the lengths it supports.
- Viewer takeaway: The person decides to begin.
- Destination and aspect ratio: Review the cut first. Export for the destination you will actually publish, in that destination's aspect ratio.
- Shot ID and intended edit length: 03. Close view, held long enough for a clean exit.
- Subject and setting: Seated person, closed notebook, piano in the same room. Same clothing, hair, and window light as shots 01 and 02.
- Authorized reference file: The approved still of this person and room. If the hand, face, or wardrobe is already wrong in the still, do not animate it.
- Character / wardrobe / product details that must stay fixed: Clothing, hair, window light, notebook position.
- Subject movement: The person takes a breath and looks toward the piano.
- One camera movement: One slow forward push, eye level. No orbit. No cut.
- Lighting and visual style: The same window light as shots 01 and 02.
- Prompt version: v1, the close-shot brief below.
- Model and settings actually selected:
- Acceptance rule (visible, checkable): The face still matches the approved still, and the expression is readable. Clothing, hair, window light, and notebook position are unchanged.
- Rejection rule: An extra person, a changed prop, a text overlay, a second camera move, or a face that no longer matches the still.
- Credit cost shown before generation:
- Maximum attempts and credit ceiling: Write both before the first render.
- Job ID:
- First / middle / last frame review: Check face, clothing, notebook, and light at each frame. The last frame needs a readable expression so the editor has a clean exit.
- Accepted file and edit notes: Download the accepted clip and trim it to the action. Watch the three shots with the sound off. Arrival, preparation, and decision should still read.

```text
Reference: the approved studio character and room.
Start: medium close view, seated character looking at the closed notebook.
Action: the character takes a breath and looks toward the piano.
Camera: one slow forward push, eye level, no orbit or cut.
End: hold the expression briefly so the editor has a clean exit.
Continuity: same clothing, hair, window light and notebook position.
Avoid: additional people, changing props, text overlays, scene transitions.
```

Adjust the wording to the controls your selected model actually has. Then inspect the clip. A prompt line is not proof that the picture obeyed it.
