# Five-user acceptance study
Recruit five founders/creators with an existing offer and their own AI account. Participation and recording consent are separate. Do not collect passwords, raw client records or unnecessary personal data. Store participant identifiers and consent outside the public repository.

Give each participant the unopened ZIP and ask them to produce one post and offer paragraph from their real brief, save the package, and explain what the toolkit does automatically. Observe without helping. After the first attempt, assistance is allowed but must be logged. An abandoned run counts as a failed first run. Do not replace participants to improve the result.

Record: participant pseudonym; host/tier/model/date; package checksum; start/end; extraction/open/copy success; source checks; intervention count/reason; saved output; unsupported claims after review; privacy errors; what they think is automated; usefulness verdict in their words; support minutes. Collect permission before quoting anyone publicly.

Pass: at least four of five complete the first output without intervention; zero unsupported factual claims survive final review; all five understand the manual boundary. Any unsupported claim or privacy leak blocks release until corrected and retested. Human judgment verifies usefulness. A script cannot establish it.

Ask willingness to buy only after they use the kit: “At €49 one-time, would you buy this version for your work?” Record yes/no/undecided and reason, separately from an actual payment. No hypothetical yes counts as revenue. Track source, checkout starts, completed payments, first-run acceptance, refunds, provider fees, VAT and support minutes. Compare ex-tax net revenue after refunds and fees against support cost. Do not claim a conversion rate without its denominator.

Current observations: zero participants recruited in this session; zero observed usability runs; sales and revenue not queried successfully. These are pending/unknown, not invented zeros for the business.
