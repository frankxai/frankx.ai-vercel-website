# The Talent Rewrite

At enterprise scale, the Talent pillar isn't about hiring. It's about portfolio management — specifically, the portfolio of decisions your people own.

Every role in an organization is a bundle of decisions. Some require judgment that can't be documented. Most don't. The Talent pillar asks: which decisions should remain with humans, and which have become automatable?

The question matters because the answer isn't static. It changes every time a new model drops. And models are dropping roughly every three days.

## The upskilling trap

The enterprise response to AI workforce anxiety is almost always the same: train everyone on AI tools. Prompting courses. ChatGPT workshops. Copilot rollouts with adoption dashboards.

This misses the problem.

The issue isn't that people lack AI skills. The issue is that most organizations haven't mapped which decisions their people are actually making — so they can't identify which ones agents should take over and which ones humans should own outright.

Training on tools without a decision audit is the wrong order. You end up with a workforce that knows how to use AI but doesn't know what to use it *for*. The Deloitte 2026 State of AI report puts the number at 80% of workers feeling unprepared for AI-reshaped roles. That statistic is real — but the standard remediation misreads it. Workers don't need more AI fluency. Most need someone to tell them which decisions they're now supposed to own exclusively.

The Talent pillar answers that question.

## The three categories

The Talent audit produces three buckets. They apply to enterprise roles and to the solo operator running ACOS.

**Rewritten.** These are tasks that look like decisions but aren't — they're documented procedures executed with effort. Research summaries. First drafts. Status reports. Code scaffolding. Scheduling. Most of what filled a 2022 knowledge worker's calendar fits here. Agents handle this now. The role that used to own it doesn't disappear — it gets refactored. The hours shift to the next two categories.

**Amplified.** These are the genuine decisions — ones that required a skilled human in 2022 and still require one in 2026, but now produce 3–5× the output per hour because the agent handles execution while the human owns judgment. Product direction. Relationship management. Architectural choices. Strategic framing. These roles scale. An amplified decision-maker with the right agent stack is a function, not just a person.

**Unnamed.** These are the new roles — the ones that exist in practice but haven't been codified into job descriptions yet. Protocol-layer architect. Agent orchestration engineer. AI Personalization Architect. The practitioner building personal AI operating systems for individuals. These roles command a $180K–$260K salary range in US markets as of mid-2026. They barely have a job description. That gap is the arbitrage.

## What the protocol layer means for Talent

The agent orchestration landscape settled into a three-layer stack this year. [MCP (Model Context Protocol)](https://onereach.ai/blog/mcp-multi-agent-ai-collaborative-intelligence/) governs tool and resource access — it crossed 200 server implementations and was donated to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation in late 2025. [A2A (Agent-to-Agent Protocol)](https://onereach.ai/blog/guide-choosing-mcp-vs-a2a-protocols/), from Google, governs how agents communicate with each other. WebMCP handles web context.

[Microsoft merged AutoGen and Semantic Kernel](https://www.morphllm.com/ai-agent-framework) into a single Agent Framework that went GA on April 3, 2026. Eight production-ready SDKs now compete at the orchestration layer.

This matters for the Talent pillar because the skill that's scarce isn't prompting — it's protocol-layer fluency. The AI Architect who can design across all three layers (tool access, agent coordination, web context) is in the Amplified category. The person who can only prompt is in the Rewritten category.

The decision that survives the rewrite: which agent to route which task to, and why. That's not a tool skill. It's judgment.

## Receipts

**The solo operator as proof of concept.** [36.3% of new ventures launched in 2026 are solo-founded](https://www.nxcode.io/resources/news/one-person-unicorn-context-engineering-solo-founder-guide-2026). Base44 — one founder, six months, 250K users — [sold to Wix for $80M in cash](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/18/6-month-old-solo-owned-vibe-coder-base44-sells-to-wix-for-80m-cash/). These aren't lifestyle businesses. They're what an Amplified operator looks like when the infrastructure is built first. The talent model isn't one person doing everything — it's one person owning all the decisions while agents handle the execution.

**The 80% problem.** [Deloitte's 2026 State of AI report](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/what-we-do/capabilities/applied-artificial-intelligence/content/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise.html): 80% of workers feel unprepared for AI-reshaped roles. 52% are actively job-hunting. The organizations responding with generic AI training are treating a decision-mapping problem as a skill-gap problem. The right diagnostic is a role audit, not a prompting course.

**The benchmark rotation.** [Claude Opus 4.8 leads the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index](https://lmcouncil.ai/benchmarks) at 61.4 as of June 2026, ahead of GPT-5.5 (60.2) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (57.0). New frontier models arrive every three days. If your Talent strategy anchors on a specific model's capabilities, it's already out of date. The stable layer is architecture, not model.

## This week's move

Run the decision audit on your own role.

List every decision you make in a typical week. Sort each one into three columns: Rewritten (documented procedure — agent handles it), Amplified (judgment call with agent execution — you own it), Unnamed (doesn't exist as a defined role yet — yours to claim).

The ratio matters. If most of what fills your week sits in the Rewritten column, you're in the highest-risk position. The move isn't panic — it's migration. Shift hours from Rewritten execution toward Amplified judgment. Build one thing in the Unnamed column.

The Talent rewrite isn't something that happens to you. It's something you can run on yourself, with the same six-pillar framework, six months before your organization figures out how to run it on you.
