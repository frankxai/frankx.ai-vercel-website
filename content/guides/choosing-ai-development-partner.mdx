Ask an AI development team what they would put in the acceptance test. How they answer tells you what they understand about the workflow, who carries the risk when the model is wrong, and what the system will cost to operate.

## Write the brief before you compare teams

Choose one workflow. Name who runs it today, the decisions it makes, the systems it touches and the cost of a wrong answer. Bring representative examples stripped of customer data, including cases people currently handle by hand.

For document extraction, agree on field-level accuracy on a held-out sample, a review path for uncertain fields, a cost ceiling per accepted document and a way to disable automation without interrupting manual processing. Set the thresholds against your baseline. A result on another dataset shows prior experience. Your own sample determines whether the design works for you.

## Match the team to the hardest part of the work

| Constraint | Evidence to request |
| --- | --- |
| Product engineering and UX | A shipped workflow, the proposed team’s role, release history and a handover walkthrough |
| Retrieval or extraction quality | A representative evaluation set, baseline, error breakdown and examples of misses or abstention |
| Sensitive data | A data-flow review, named subprocessors, access boundaries, deletion and incident procedures |
| Production ownership | Client-controlled repository and deployments, logs, rollback and cost reporting |

A vendor-authored case is a starting point. Look for a client account of the same work, then ask a reference what the proposed team personally delivered. Request names and availability for the people who would work with you. A platform badge helps you check ecosystem alignment. Staffing and delivery fit take their own evidence.

## Use a paid pilot to turn claims into evidence

Give the team a narrow scope, one agreed dataset, a spending cap and a deadline. Name the person who signs off and agree on the work they will inspect. Ask for source code, evaluation results, deployment and rollback instructions, and a record of open risks.

At the end, choose a production increment, a revised test or a stop. You should be able to make that decision and keep the work you paid for when your delivery team changes.

## Keep advice and delivery economics visible

Independent architecture work can define the brief, review a shortlist or assess delivery milestones. Each implementation team should give you its own scope and terms. Ask advisers to disclose any referral compensation before an introduction and explain their recommendation against your requirements. Give explicit permission before any adviser shares your brief with a provider.
