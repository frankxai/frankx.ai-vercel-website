A founder's week fills with meetings that exist to repeat one update to different people: investors, the team, customers, a prospect who missed the call. A short recorded video moves that update onto the viewer's schedule and gives you the hour back. This guide is for founders and CEOs who want five recurring videos, recorded in one 30-minute block a week, with preparation the night before and a review pass before sending. You get a structure and a mini script for each, the calendar block, an honest line on AI avatars, a tool table and a privacy checklist. The smallest next move: pick the meeting you repeat most this week and replace it with a three-minute recording, planned with the [free script template](/downloads/demo-video-script-template.md).

## When a recorded update beats a meeting

Record it when the information flows one way, when several people need the same thing, when a screen explains it faster than words, or when time zones make a live slot expensive. Keep it live when the point is a decision that needs debate, a negotiation, or bad news for one person. A video can set up that conversation beforehand.

A simple test: if nobody would interrupt you during the meeting, it can be a video.

## Five videos a founder can send

| Video | Audience | Length | Cadence | Layout |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Investor update | Investors and advisors | 2 to 4 minutes | Monthly | Screen for charts, camera for the ask |
| Team weekly | Whole company | 3 to 5 minutes | Weekly | Camera with screen when showing work |
| Customer or product update | Customers, or one account | 1 to 2 minutes | Per release or monthly | Screen with camera bubble |
| Sales follow-up | One prospect | 60 to 120 seconds | After each call | Camera to open and close, screen in the middle |
| Hiring and culture | Candidates | 2 to 3 minutes | Per open role | Camera large throughout |

Square brackets in the scripts below are placeholders. Replace them with your own facts before recording.

### 1. Investor update

**Structure:** three numbers, one win, one problem, one ask. The written update stays the record, because numbers in text can be searched and forwarded. The video adds your voice and your judgment on top.

**Mini script:**

> "Hi all, this is the [month] update. Three minutes. Three numbers first: revenue [figure], burn [figure], runway [months]. The win: [one sentence, with the customer or metric that proves it]. The problem: [one sentence], and here is what we are doing about it: [one sentence]. The ask: [specific, such as two introductions to heads of finance at Series B SaaS companies]. Reply to this email with a name. Thank you."

Show the chart on screen during the numbers, then switch to camera for the ask. Send it behind a password. An unlisted link can be forwarded, so it is not enough for revenue, burn or runway.

### 2. Team weekly

**Structure:** what shipped, what is next, one decision with an owner and a date, one specific thank-you.

**Mini script:**

> "Week [number]. What shipped: [two items, with who built them]. What is next: [the one priority for this week]. One decision: we are [decision], [name] owns it, and it is done by [date]. If you disagree, reply in [channel] by Wednesday. Thank you to [name] for [specific thing]. That is the week."

The disagreement deadline matters. It turns a broadcast into a decision the team can still challenge.

### 3. Customer or product update

**Structure:** one change, shown working, why it matters to this customer, what they should do now.

**Mini script:**

> "Hi [name or team]. One change this month: [feature], and here it is. [Show it on screen in under 40 seconds.] For you, this means [specific outcome tied to how they use the product]. To turn it on, [one step]. Reply here if anything looks off."

For a single key account, record it per account and use their data in a demo copy. For everyone, keep it generic and put it in the release email.

### 4. Sales follow-up

**Structure:** their words from the call, the one part of the product they asked about, the next meeting.

**Mini script:**

> "Hi [name], thanks for today. You said [their exact phrase about the problem]. Here is the part you asked about. [Show only that workflow.] That is the step that [their outcome]. If it fits, pick a slot for [next step] with the button under this video."

Record the opening and close fresh for each prospect and reuse the product middle. The [product demo video guide](/guides/product-demo-video-guide) has a full 90-second example with a shot list.

### 5. Hiring and culture

**Structure:** who you are, what the role owns in its first six months, how the team works, one honest hard part, how to apply.

**Mini script:**

> "I'm [name], founder of [company]. We are hiring a [role]. In the first six months you will own [two concrete outcomes]. We work [how: async, in-office days, review rhythm]. The hard part, honestly: [one real constraint]. If that still sounds right, apply at [link], and I read every application."

Only promise "I read every application" if it is true.

## The weekly 30-minute recording block

This is a proposed schedule to adapt; it has not been measured. Put one fixed 30-minute slot in the calendar, the same day and time every week. Prepare the night before: five bullet points per video, pasted into speaker notes. Tella has a teleprompter and records in separate clips, so each video can be re-recorded without touching the others.

| Minute | Do | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Run the privacy checklist below, open the notes, test the mic for five seconds | A clean screen and a working mic |
| 5 to 10 | Record the team weekly | One 3 to 5 minute video |
| 10 to 14 | Record the customer update when there is a release; otherwise one more sales follow-up | One 1 to 2 minute video |
| 14 to 20 | Record two or three sales follow-ups from this week's calls | Two or three 60 to 120 second videos |
| 20 to 25 | First week of the month: the investor update. Other weeks: a hiring video or a reshoot | One video |
| 25 to 30 | Cut filler by transcript and check captions | Every video ready for review |

Recording is only part of the work: plan time after the block to watch each export once and send it. Most weeks this schedule aims at four to six videos. Investor and hiring videos rotate in, so no single week carries all five.

Two rules keep the block at 30 minutes. First, one take per clip unless you misspoke a number or a name. Second, the edit is a cut pass only: transcript cuts, filler and silence removal, captions. Save zoom choreography and B-roll for product demos.

## Real face or AI avatar

Synthesia and HeyGen generate a presenter from a script. They do not record your product or your screen, and both meter use with credits. Free tiers are small: Synthesia's Basic plan gives 10 minutes a month with a watermark, and HeyGen's free plan gives 3 videos a month up to 1 minute each (both checked 20 October 2026 on [Synthesia's pricing page](https://www.synthesia.io/pricing) and [HeyGen's pricing page](https://www.heygen.com/pricing)). Much of the advice online about avatars for executives is published by companies that sell avatars.

My view, by situation:

| Situation | Real recording | Avatar acceptable | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Investor update | Yes | No | Investors are judging your judgment |
| Bad news: missed targets, layoffs, price changes | Yes | No | A generated face reads as avoidance |
| Hiring and culture | Yes | No | The candidate is deciding whether to work with you |
| First sales follow-up after a call | Yes | No | The prospect just met you |
| Translated version of a message you recorded yourself | Preferred | Yes, disclosed | The message is yours; the avatar carries the language |
| Internal training or policy explainer | Either | Yes, disclosed | The information matters more than the messenger |
| Draft to test a script before you record it | No | Yes, internal only | Nobody outside sees it |

Disclose an avatar in the video and in the message. One line is enough: "This video uses an AI-generated presenter reading a script [name] wrote."

There is a middle ground: AI that edits a real recording. Tella added eye-contact correction on 9 October 2026, and transcript cuts, Studio Voice audio cleanup and filler removal all change a real take. My rule: if the AI changed what you said or who appears to say it, disclose it. If it trimmed pauses or cleaned the audio, you do not need to.

## Privacy: what never goes on screen

A screen recording shows everything in the frame, and viewers pause. Run this before every block.

**Close or hide:**

- Email, Slack and other chat apps, including their notification badges
- Calendars showing other people's names or meetings
- Password managers, browser autofill and saved-login prompts
- Terminal history, `.env` files, API keys and tokens
- Bookmarks bar, open tabs and file paths that name clients
- Customer names and data in the product. Use a demo account with invented data.
- Cap tables, bank balances and payroll, unless the video is a password-protected investor update that needs them
- Anything about candidates in a hiring video

**Set up once:**

- A separate browser profile for recording, with no extensions you would not show
- Do-not-disturb mode switched on at the start of the block
- A demo workspace with realistic but invented data

**Before you send:**

- Watch the export full screen once and look only at the edges of the frame, where notifications and tabs hide.
- Blur anything you missed. Tella has blur and highlighting in its editor.
- Match the sharing setting to the audience: password protection for investors and anything confidential, and search-engine indexing switched off. Tella Premium's domain restriction limits which websites can embed a video; it does not control who can watch a shared link.
