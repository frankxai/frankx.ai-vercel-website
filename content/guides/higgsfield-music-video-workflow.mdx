Build the music video's edit around the approved audio master. Mark the track's transitions, choose a visual idea that fits the song and generate only the motion shots needed to carry it.

This guide provides an original production method for an artist or creator with a track they are authorized to use. It does not claim that a video generator will precisely synchronize every movement to your waveform or deliver a finished film from one prompt.

## Choose the release artifact first

| Artifact | What it needs | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Full music video | A visual arc across the track | Repeating one clip without a reason |
| Visualizer | One coherent moving world | Too much action competing with the song |
| Vertical teaser | A selected musical phrase and clear opening | Cropping away the subject |
| Lyric clip | Accurate words and readable timing | Asking video generation to render the lyrics |

Your distribution destination determines composition, duration and final encoding. Check its current upload requirements directly. Keep a clean master and make the platform versions from it.

## Map the song before generating

Import the approved track into your editor. Mark intro, verse, chorus, bridge and ending as applicable. Then mark the few moments where a visual change matters: a vocal entrance, harmonic shift, drum drop or return to quiet.

Beat spacing can be estimated as `60 / BPM` seconds. At 120 BPM, one beat lasts 0.5 seconds and four beats last 2 seconds. Real recordings can change tempo or include expressive timing; use the actual waveform and listening judgment for final cuts.

Do not cut every beat automatically. A sustained phrase can benefit from a held composition while a transition can earn a new view.

## Define a visual arc

Example proposal: **a pianist moves from a quiet studio into an imagined coastal landscape, then returns to the original room**. One location change corresponds to the track's central lift. One recurring character gives the viewer continuity.

| Section | Visual job | Production approach |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | Establish room and atmosphere | Stable reference and small motion |
| First development | Introduce the character | Simple glance or preparation |
| Main lift | Expand scale | New wide environment with matching palette |
| Quiet return | Restore intimacy | Reuse the approved room reference |
| Ending | Resolve the visual idea | Held composition and clean exit |

This is a narrative template, not an account of an existing FrankX music video. Change it to match your song's emotional structure.

## Lock references before motion

Prepare a character pack, wardrobe reference, room reference and landscape reference. Use the [continuity workflow](/guides/higgsfield-soul-id-character-consistency) for recurring human or stylized characters.

For an instrument-performance scene, visible hand movement must fit the performance you want to imply. Generated fingers may not match the music or physical playing technique. Use your own recorded performance when that accuracy matters; reserve generated scenes for atmosphere and illustration.

## Write one action per shot

```text
Shot MV-03: coastal expansion.
Approved references: character-v1, outfit-v1, coastline-v1.
Composition: wide view; character small in frame facing the sea.
Action: wind moves the coat slightly; the character remains still.
Camera: one restrained forward movement, no cut or orbit.
Light: retain the approved evening direction and palette.
End: hold the final composition for an edit point.
No text, new people or wardrobe changes.
```

Use supported duration and resolution settings in the generation form. The beat map controls your final edit; it is not proof that the model will obey exact musical timing.

## Assemble to the actual track

Lay the original audio master down first. Place accepted footage against your marked sections. Trim motion to the usable range. Use cut points, dissolves or held frames when they serve the musical phrase.

Add lyrics as separate typography in the editor. Proofread them against the approved lyric sheet. Make a vertical composition separately when the wide master cannot be cropped without losing the story.

Review the whole export without stopping. Check the audio begins and ends as intended, transitions feel deliberate, repeated shots remain purposeful and the character survives the sequence.

## Budget by musical section

Allocate a small test budget to the hardest shot before spreading credits across the track. Reuse accepted stills and locations. Choose a held frame or recorded performance when it communicates better than another render.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can Higgsfield make a complete music video from an MP3?

Use the current workflow's actual input requirements. A track, references and an edit plan remain separate production assets. This guide makes no one-click completion claim.

### Will generated piano or guitar playing match the song?

Inspect it carefully. When technique and synchronization matter, record the performance and use generated imagery for the surrounding scenes.

### How do I make a lyric video?

Generate the backgrounds and motion as needed, then set the approved lyrics and timing in the editor. Keep text separate so spelling, readability and revisions stay under your control.
