A product demo video has one job: show one specific person their problem getting solved, quickly enough that they keep watching. Most demos lose that person before the product appears, with a logo, a company story or a tour of every menu. This guide is for founders, sales and customer success teams, and product marketers who need one demo that works on a landing page, in a follow-up email and inside onboarding. You will leave with a filled-in 90-second script, a shot list, a recording setup, an editing pass and a review checklist. The smallest honest next move: download the [free demo video script template](/downloads/demo-video-script-template.md) and fill in its one-page brief before you open any recorder.

## First decide: watch, click or read

Search results for "best screen recorder for product demos" mix video recorders with interactive demo builders, and the two answer different questions. Pick the format before the tool. One question settles most cases: **will the viewer watch, click, or follow along later?**

| The viewer needs to | Format | Tools to consider | Use it for | Skip it when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hear why it matters and see it work | Narrated video | Tella, Screen Studio, Loom | Landing pages, sales follow-ups, launches, release notes | The viewer must learn clicks they will repeat later |
| Try the product by clicking | Interactive demo | Arcade, Supademo, Storylane, Navattic | "See it" buttons on your site, self-serve evaluation | Your explanation carries the value, or the UI is thin |
| Follow steps later at their own pace | Written guide with screenshots | Scribe | Internal processes, support articles, SOPs | The reader still needs persuading |
| Receive a scripted message without a presenter on camera | AI avatar video | Synthesia, HeyGen | Translated variants, training explainers | The viewer is deciding whether to trust you |

The avatar row answers a separate question: does a real person need to be on screen? Avatar tools generate the presenter. They do not record your product, so a demo still needs real screen footage underneath. The [async video guide for founders](/guides/async-video-for-founders) covers when an avatar is acceptable.

## The five-block structure

Every block is short enough to re-record on its own. The template has the full version; here is the shape.

1. **The problem in their words (0 to 10 seconds).** Say it the way the viewer would. No "hi, I'm", no company history.
2. **Where we are (10 to 20 seconds).** One sentence so the screen makes sense.
3. **The job getting done (20 to 70 seconds).** One workflow, start to finish, with realistic data. Cut every click that does not move the job forward.
4. **The proof moment (a few seconds).** Stop. Zoom on the result. Say what changed in one sentence.
5. **One next step (last 5 to 10 seconds).** One link, one reply or one calendar slot.

Length depends on where the video lives. These are the targets I recommend:

| Placement | Target length |
|---|---|
| Landing page or launch | 60 to 90 seconds |
| Sales follow-up or personal reply | 60 to 120 seconds |
| Onboarding walkthrough | 2 to 5 minutes per task |
| Investor or team update | 2 to 4 minutes |
| Course lesson or tutorial | 5 to 12 minutes per lesson |

## Worked example: a 75-second demo for a fictional B2B tool

The product below is invented so the example stays clean. Tallyhook reads a support team's help desk tickets and builds a weekly list of customer accounts at risk of churning. Swap in your own product and keep the structure.

### The one-page brief

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| Viewer | Head of customer success at a 40-person SaaS company |
| What they are trying to get done | Know which accounts are at risk before the Monday pipeline call |
| What they use today | A Friday spreadsheet export from the help desk, sorted by hand |
| The one claim this video proves | Tallyhook builds the at-risk list from tickets with no manual sorting |
| The proof moment | One account flagged, with the three tickets that triggered the flag |
| The one action | Connect the help desk and get the first report tonight |
| Target length | 75 seconds, inside the 90-second ceiling |
| Where it will be watched | Landing page, and the first follow-up email after a demo request |

### The script

**0:00 to 0:08, camera large.** "If you build your at-risk account list by hand every Friday, these 75 seconds replace it."

**0:08 to 0:15, screen with camera bubble.** "This is Tallyhook, connected to a help desk with last month's tickets. Nobody sorted anything here."

**0:15 to 0:50, screen with camera bubble.** "Monday's report is already built. Accounts are ranked by risk. I'll open the top one, Brightwell Dental. Tallyhook grouped three tickets from the last six days, all about invoices. I assign it to the account manager, add a note, and it shows up in their queue."

**0:50 to 1:00, screen only, zoom on the flag.** "Three invoice tickets in six days. A weekly spreadsheet misses that pattern until the renewal call."

**1:00 to 1:15, camera large.** "Connect your help desk and your first report builds tonight. The link is right under this video."

### The shot list

| # | Time | On screen | What you say | Layout | Edit note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00 to 0:08 | Your face | The Friday problem | Camera large | Start on the first word, no intro |
| 2 | 0:08 to 0:15 | Tallyhook dashboard, help desk connected | Where we are | Screen with camera bubble | Browser zoom 125% |
| 3 | 0:15 to 0:32 | Monday report, ranked list | Report is built, ranked by risk | Screen with camera bubble | Cut the page load |
| 4 | 0:32 to 0:50 | Brightwell Dental detail, assign and note | Three tickets, assign, note | Screen with camera bubble | One zoom on the ticket group |
| 5 | 0:50 to 1:00 | The flag and its three tickets | The proof sentence | Screen only | Hold the zoom 3 seconds |
| 6 | 1:00 to 1:15 | Your face | The one action | Camera large | CTA button on the share page |

### Why this script works

- The first sentence names the viewer's Friday chore. The viewer recognises themselves before seeing any product.
- The middle shows one workflow. Settings, integrations and the pricing page stay out.
- The data looks real: a named account, dated tickets, a specific pattern. Placeholder data reads as a mockup.
- The proof moment gets its own clip, so you can re-record it until it lands.
- The ask is a single action that matches the claim.

## Recording setup

### Screen and data

- Use a separate browser profile with no bookmarks bar, no extensions you would not show, and only the tabs in the shot list.
- Seed a demo account with realistic data and invented customer names. Never record a real customer's account.
- Set browser zoom to 110 to 125 percent so text survives a small embedded player.
- Size the window to the frame: 16:9 for most placements, 9:16 if you plan a social cut.
- Turn off notifications for the system, Slack, mail and calendar.

### Voice and camera

- Record five seconds and listen back before the real take.
- Put light in front of you. A window behind you turns your face into a silhouette.
- Keep the script where your eyes naturally fall, close to the camera. Tella has a built-in teleprompter and speaker notes, which keep your eyes near the lens.

### Record in clips

Record each shot-list row as its own clip. A mistake then costs one clip. Tella records in multiple clips natively; in other tools, pause between rows and cut later. Record the proof moment twice.

### Which recorder on which machine

- **Mac:** Screen Studio or Tella. Screen Studio is macOS only, and its FAQ says there are no near-future plans for Windows.
- **Windows:** Tella, Loom, Cap or FocuSee all run natively.
- **Browser only:** Tella's web recorder or Chrome extension, or Loom's Chrome extension.

## The editing pass: cut, zoom, captions

Do these in order. Each step depends on the one before.

1. **Cut by transcript.** Delete words in the transcript and the video follows. Remove filler words, silences, false starts and every click that does not move the job. Tella has this on its paid plans, plus Auto Cut. Screen Studio has transcript editing with filler and pause removal. Loom puts filler removal and edit-by-transcript on its Business + AI plan.
2. **Zoom on what matters.** Let auto zoom make the first pass (Tella's Auto Zoom has been adaptive by default since 21 August 2026; Screen Studio zooms on clicks). Then review every zoom by hand. Keep the one on the proof moment, hold it long enough to read, and delete zooms that land on empty space.
3. **Clean the audio.** Tella's Studio Voice removes noise and echo. Any equivalent works; a demo with room echo sounds cheap no matter how good the picture is.
4. **Caption last.** Captions follow the final transcript, so they come after the cut. Viewers who start with sound off depend on captions for the first seconds. Check that your product name and your customers' terms are spelled right. Tella generates captions in 106 languages and exports SRT files.
5. **Choose the first frame.** The thumbnail or first frame should show the result. A logo on frame one tells the viewer nothing.

The goal of the pass is a video at or under its target length, where every second either shows the job or proves it.

## Where the demo goes

One recording session can feed four placements. Change the cut and the call to action for each.

| Placement | Length | What changes | What to measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landing page | 60 to 90 seconds | Embed near the main button, captions on, result in the first frame | Plays, and where viewers drop off |
| Email | Same cut | Link a thumbnail or animated GIF to the share page; the subject line names the outcome | Clicks, then watch time |
| Sales follow-up | 60 to 120 seconds, recorded per prospect | Open with the prospect's own words from the call; show only what they asked about; the share page button books the next meeting | Whether they watched, and how far |
| Onboarding | 2 to 5 minutes per task | One task per video, titled as the task ("Connect your help desk"); link the next task at the end | Completion per task |

For the sales follow-up, record a fresh opening and close for each prospect and reuse the product middle. Tella sends view notifications, so you can follow up with the people who watched first. For onboarding, Tella videos embed in Intercom help centres.

Read the numbers once a week. If most viewers leave before the proof moment, move the proof earlier or cut the setup. Tella's analytics show views, watch time and drop-off, with 30 days of history on Pro and all-time history on Premium.

## Review checklist before you share

Watch the export twice: once with sound off, once at 1.5x speed.

- [ ] The problem is clear in the first 10 seconds.
- [ ] The proof moment arrives within the first minute and is zoomed and held.
- [ ] A viewer with sound off can follow it, and captions spell your product and customer terms correctly.
- [ ] There is exactly one call to action, and the share page button points to the right URL.
- [ ] No private data on screen: no tokens, customer names, internal URLs, inbox, Slack or calendar.
- [ ] The title says what the viewer gets. The first frame shows the result.
- [ ] The link plays in a private browser window while you are logged out.
- [ ] The length is at or under the target for its placement.
- [ ] One person outside the team watched it and could say what the product does in one sentence.

If any line fails, fix that clip and re-export. Short clips make this a five-minute job.
