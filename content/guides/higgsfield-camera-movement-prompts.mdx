A camera prompt should define where the shot begins, how the view changes and where it ends. Start with one move. A short controlled shot is easier to review and cut than a prompt asking for a push, orbit, zoom and transition at once.

These are original exercise briefs, not tested success-rate claims. The terminology follows ordinary cinematography. Match each brief to the controls actually available in your chosen Higgsfield model or studio.

## Pan, dolly and zoom describe different changes

| Move | What changes | Useful job | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pan | Camera rotates horizontally from a fixed position | Reveal a wider setting | Horizon drifts or objects stretch |
| Tilt | Camera rotates vertically | Reveal height or scale | Perspective warps during the move |
| Dolly / push | Camera translates toward or away from the subject | Bring attention closer | Subject grows inconsistently |
| Truck | Camera translates sideways | Follow a subject or reveal depth | Background slides without parallax |
| Orbit | Camera moves around the subject | Show another side | Hidden details are invented |
| Zoom | Framing changes through focal-length behavior | Emphasize a detail | It becomes a dolly or an unexpected cut |
| Locked shot | Camera remains fixed | Make action legible | Unrequested motion takes over |

A dolly changes viewpoint and perspective; a zoom changes framing from the same viewpoint. A dolly zoom combines translation and focal-length change to keep subject size approximately stable while perspective changes. It is a demanding effect; use a simple subject and inspect the result.

Higgsfield's [camera-control guide](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/ai-video-camera-control) explains prompt and interface-based direction. Its current documentation should determine your parameter choices; the table above describes cinematographic intent.

## A prompt structure you can reuse

```text
Subject and approved reference:
Start framing and camera height:
One camera move and its direction:
One scene action:
Speed intention:
End framing and hold:
Continuity: light, outfit, location and props:
Unwanted changes:
```

Technical settings belong in the interface when it exposes them. Do not put contradictory settings in the prompt. Words such as “slow” are creative instructions, not guaranteed numeric speed controls.

## Prompt 1: a locked product demonstration

```text
Use the approved product reference in the same studio setting.
Fixed medium view at product height. Camera stays locked.
One authorized presenter lifts the lid once, then pauses.
Keep the product shape, hand contact and window light consistent.
End with the opened product visible. No cut, orbit or text overlay.
```

Review contact and product geometry. When the hand action fails, simplify to a product-only still with a slight lighting or camera change. Put exact branding and interface text into the final edit using real assets.

## Prompt 2: a slow push toward a character

```text
Use the approved seated-character reference.
Start at a medium eye-level view.
One slow forward camera push toward a closer portrait.
The character looks up once and then holds a calm expression.
Keep the face, outfit, room and key light consistent.
No cut, new object, orbit or abrupt speed change.
```

Compare the opening, middle and end frames with the reference. A successful beginning does not prove the face survives the move.

## Prompt 3: a lateral reveal

```text
Use the approved room reference with a piano by the window.
Start partly behind the doorframe at eye level.
Camera moves gently sideways to reveal the instrument.
The room stays still except for slight curtain movement.
End with the piano and window readable in one composition.
Keep architecture, reflections and light direction consistent.
```

Look for coherent parallax and stable room geometry. When the camera seems to slide a flat picture, reduce the movement or create a reference with clearer foreground and background depth.

## Prompt 4: a restrained orbit

```text
Use the approved full-body character and outfit references.
Begin at a three-quarter front view at chest height.
Make one small orbit toward a side view; the subject stays still.
Keep face, body proportions, outfit details and background consistent.
End at the side view with a brief hold. No full revolution or cut.
```

A full revolution exposes unseen details and raises the continuity burden. Provide the side and back references when the model supports them. Use the [character guide](/guides/higgsfield-soul-id-character-consistency) to prepare those assets.

## Diagnose before another render

| What you see | Change first | What to hold constant |
|---|---|---|
| Unexpected cuts | Ask for one continuous shot; use a supported single-shot option | Subject and reference |
| Multiple competing moves | Keep one movement | Scene action |
| Face changes during orbit | Supply the missing angle or reduce the orbit | Outfit and light |
| Camera and subject both move badly | Lock one of them | Framing and location |
| Poor final composition | Define the end frame explicitly | Start reference |

Record each change. A batch where prompt, reference, model and duration all change together teaches you little about the failure.
