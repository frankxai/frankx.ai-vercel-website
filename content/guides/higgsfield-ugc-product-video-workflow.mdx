A useful UGC-style ad shows a product solving one recognizable problem. Build the script around a demonstration you can substantiate, then use a synthetic presenter or generated scene where it serves that demonstration.

This is a proposed production workflow. “UGC-style” describes the visual format; generated actors are not real customers. Keep that distinction visible and never write a fictional customer experience as a testimonial.

## Separate the claim from the presentation

Write the product claim before choosing a face or effect. For software, capture the actual screen and result. For a physical product, preserve its real shape, label and functioning. Generated lifestyle footage can establish context, but it should not invent what the product does.

| Layer | Input you need | Rejection condition |
|---|---|---|
| Claim | Product documentation or demonstrated result | Unsupported promise |
| Proof | Actual screen, product action or authorized source | Fabricated interface or outcome |
| Presenter | Original character or authorized likeness and voice | Implied real customer experience |
| Setting | Reference matching the intended audience context | Scene obscures the product |
| CTA | Working destination that continues the promise | Landing page asks for something unrelated |

## Write a 20–30-second editorial brief

| Beat | What to say or show | Editorial target |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Name one familiar task or frustration | First 3–5 seconds |
| Demonstration | Show the actual operation or product action | Next 8–12 seconds |
| Meaning | Explain what the result changes for the viewer | Next 4–6 seconds |
| Next step | One clear choice | Final 3–5 seconds |

The ranges are planning targets, not generator limits. Approve the spoken script and proof assets first, then choose a supported duration.

Original script example for a planning tool:

```text
Opening: "Here is the shot list for this video."
Demonstration: show the actual project with three approved reference frames.
Explanation: "Each shot has one action, one camera move and a review rule."
Next step: "Use the free worksheet to plan your first three shots."
```

This example makes a visible claim about a worksheet. Replace it with your real product and destination. Avoid invented time savings or income outcomes.

## Prepare the product and presenter references

Use clean product images from several meaningful angles. Include a detail image when a connector, label or mechanism matters. Supply the exact assets for logos and text in the edit rather than expecting the model to reproduce them reliably.

For the presenter, build the [character reference pack](/guides/higgsfield-soul-id-character-consistency). Define tone, wardrobe and setting before generating variants. A recognizable recurring presenter can link episodes, but each episode must still earn attention through the lesson.

## Generate a limited first draft

```text
Create one UGC-style product explainer using the supplied product assets,
approved fictional presenter and approved script.
Keep the product shape and meaningful details consistent.
Use simple medium framing and one clear demonstration.
Do not add testimonials, performance numbers, logos or pricing claims.
Before submission, report supported duration, aspect ratio and credit cost.
Stop after one draft and return the job ID and original output.
```

This is a proposed agent brief. Use the workflow and tools available in your account; do not substitute an unrelated preset because it has a similar name.

## Inspect the draft against the product

Play the clip slowly around hand contact and product manipulation. Compare the product with its reference. Listen for mispronounced names, invented lines and overstated claims. Remove a scene that changes the physical product even when the presenter looks convincing.

Make the actual demonstration the center of the edit. Keep captions readable, voice intelligible and the final CTA aligned with the destination. Add a synthetic-presenter label when viewers could otherwise interpret the footage as real testimony.

## Test one variation at a time

Start with two openings around the same demonstration: show the product result first, or name the problem first. Keep the presenter, claim, proof and destination constant. Record the distribution conditions and chosen metric before publishing.

Use retention to examine viewing behavior and qualified visits to examine intent. Organic variation alone does not establish causation. Paid distribution needs its own approved budget and publication controls.

## Keep a reusable delivery package

Save the approved script, original product references, presenter version, generation record, clean master, caption file and final platform exports. That package supports revisions when the product changes and prevents an outdated claim from surviving in a reused ad.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a generated presenter real UGC?

It is synthetic creative in a UGC-style format. Present it honestly and do not attribute experiences to a nonexistent customer.

### Can I generate exact product labels and UI screens?

Inspect every meaningful detail. For text, logos and software behavior, use real product assets and screen recordings in the edit.

### Should I produce dozens of variants immediately?

First approve one complete draft and one limited variation test. Increase the batch only after the product, claim and editing method work.
