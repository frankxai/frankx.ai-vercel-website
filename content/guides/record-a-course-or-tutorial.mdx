This guide gives you a setup for three budgets, a lesson structure, a way to record in clips so updates stay cheap, step-by-step slide recording, a method for turning an article you already wrote into a lesson, and a tool pick for each job. ## The short version

1. Write the lesson outcome in one sentence before anything else.
2. Split the lesson into three to five chapters. Each chapter becomes one clip.
3. Keep each lesson between 5 and 12 minutes.
4. Spend money on sound before you spend it on a camera.
5. Record chapters as separate clips, in batches, with notes near the camera.
6. Edit by transcript, correct the captions, publish with chapters.

## Setup by budget

These are rough tiers. Prices vary by region and sale, so treat the headings as rough budgets and buy by category.

| Part | Built-in or free | Around $100 | Around $300 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Microphone | The headset or earbud mic you own, held close and tested | A USB microphone on a desk arm, 15 to 20 cm from your mouth | A better USB or XLR microphone with a small audio interface |
| Camera | Laptop webcam, raised to eye level on books | A 1080p external webcam on top of the monitor | A 4K webcam, or a camera you already own used as a webcam |
| Light | A window in front of you, never behind | One LED panel or key light beside the monitor | Two lights: key light plus a softer fill on the other side |
| Room | Soft furnishings, door closed, fridge and fan off | Rug and curtains to cut echo | Acoustic panels behind and beside you |
| Software | OBS Studio (free) or your recorder's trial | A recorder with transcript editing and hosting | Same, plus a teleprompter or a second screen for notes |
| Extras | Water, phone face down | Second monitor for speaker notes | Microphone arm and camera mount so nothing moves between sessions |

I put sound first because echo and hiss are harder to sit through than a soft image. A cheap microphone placed close beats an expensive one across the room. Light comes second; a window in front of your face is free and often enough.

## Structure each lesson around one outcome

Give each lesson one outcome. State it first, in the viewer's words, then deliver it in chapters that each move them one step.

| Block | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | "By the end of this lesson you can..." Camera large. | 20 to 30 seconds |
| Context | Where we are and what the viewer needs open. | 30 to 60 seconds |
| Chapters 1 to 3 (or up to 5) | One step each, shown on screen with real material. | 1 to 3 minutes each |
| Proof | The finished result on screen, held for a few seconds. | 10 to 20 seconds |
| Recap and next lesson | One-sentence recap, one link to the next lesson. Camera large. | 20 to 30 seconds |

Three rules keep this honest:

- **5 to 12 minutes per lesson.** If you are past 12, look for the second outcome hiding inside and split it.
- **Chapters are your edit points and your update points.** Name them as tasks ("Tag the quotes"), never as topics ("Tagging").
- **The title says what the viewer gets.** "Turn five interviews into a ranked feature list" beats "Lesson 4: Synthesis".

## Record in clips, then batch

Recording a whole lesson in one take means one stumble at minute nine costs you nine minutes. Recording in clips means a mistake costs one clip. It also changes what an update costs: when the software you teach changes a menu, you re-record the one clip that shows it and keep the rest.

A batch routine that works for a module of five to eight lessons:

1. **Plan the module on paper first.** Every lesson gets an outcome sentence and a chapter list. Number clips as lesson and chapter, for example 3.2.
2. **Record all camera-only clips in one session.** Intros, recaps and module welcomes share the same light, shirt and framing, so they cut together cleanly.
3. **Record all screen chapters in a second session.** Same browser profile, same zoom level, same demo account.
4. **Read from notes near the lens.** Tella has a teleprompter for speaker notes on Mac and a Speaker Notes window in its Windows app; a second monitor directly under the camera works with any recorder.
5. **Edit by transcript.** Delete the bad takes as text. Tella, Descript and Camtasia (Essentials tier and up) all offer transcript-based editing with filler-word removal.
6. **Keep a re-record log.** When a product updates, list the clips that show the changed screen. That list is your update job.

Tella records multiple clips into one video and supports reusable clips, which is the mechanism this workflow depends on. Any recorder that saves separate files works too; you then join the clips in an editor.

## Record yourself presenting slides

The method below works the same in Google Slides, PowerPoint and Keynote because the recording happens in your screen recorder. You also get transcript editing, captions and a share page that the built-in recorders may lack.

### Step by step, for any deck

1. **Prepare the deck for video.** One idea per slide, large text (a phone viewer should read it), and speaker notes written as spoken sentences.
2. **Set your speaker notes near the camera.** Use presenter view on a second screen placed under the webcam, or your recorder's teleprompter.
3. **Start the slideshow.** On one monitor, run it in a window so you can still reach the recorder; on two monitors, run it full screen on the screen you record.
4. **Choose screen plus camera in your recorder.** Put your camera in a corner bubble that does not cover slide text. Use a full-camera layout for the opening and the recap.
5. **Record one clip per section.** Pause between sections, advance the slide, and start the next clip.
6. **Edit and caption.** Remove pauses in the transcript, fix names and terms in the captions, and add chapters that match your section titles.

### Notes per tool

- **Google Slides.** 9to5Google reported that Google Slides has a built-in recording feature with a 30-minute limit per recording. That limit comes from a single report, so check it in your own account before you plan a long lecture around it.
- **PowerPoint and Keynote.** Both can record a presentation from inside the app. I have not re-checked their current menus for this guide, so follow the vendor's help page if you prefer the built-in route, and use the method above when you want transcript editing and hosting.
- **Embedding the result back into slides.** Tella supports embeds in Google Docs, Slides and Sheets, so a recorded explanation can sit inside the deck you share.

## Turn an article or newsletter into a recorded lesson

If you write, the research and argument for a lesson usually exist already. What remains is cutting a piece written for reading into one built for listening.

1. **Pick a piece that teaches one thing.** Essays and opinion pieces convert badly. How-to issues convert well.
2. **Rewrite the headline as an outcome.** "Why interviews mislead" becomes "Turn five interviews into a ranked feature list".
3. **Cut to three steps.** Keep the steps, drop the digressions. Move the best digression into a separate short video.
4. **Rewrite paragraphs as speaker notes.** Spoken sentences are shorter. Read each one aloud; if you run out of breath, split it.
5. **Decide what is on screen for each step.** A slide for the idea, a live document or tool for the doing, your face for the opening and the recap.
6. **Publish both ways.** The article becomes the lesson notes and the text version for anyone who prefers to read. Link the video from the article and the article from the video description.

### Worked example: one newsletter issue to a 7-minute lesson

A hypothetical 1,400-word issue on customer interviews, converted with the steps above.

| Time | Chapter | On screen | Layout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:30 | Outcome: "By the end you can turn five interview transcripts into a ranked feature list." | Your face | Camera large |
| 0:30 to 1:30 | Why raw notes mislead | Two slides | Slides plus camera bubble |
| 1:30 to 4:00 | Tag the quotes | A shared document with three sample transcripts | Screen plus camera bubble |
| 4:00 to 6:30 | Rank by frequency and pain | A spreadsheet with the tags counted | Screen only, zoom on the ranked list |
| 6:30 to 7:00 | Recap and next lesson | Your face | Camera large |

Five clips. If the spreadsheet tool changes its interface, chapter 4 is the only clip to re-record. The original issue ships as the lesson notes.

## Captions and accessibility

Captions are part of the lesson. People watch with the sound off, viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing depend on them, and anyone learning in a second language reads along.

- **Turn captions on and correct them.** Auto transcription needs a human pass for names, product terms and jargon. Fix those in the transcript before you publish.
- **Know what your tool gives you.** Tella lists captions in 106 languages that you can edit, translate, burn into the video or download as SRT. Cap generates captions locally with Whisper, free. Descript and Camtasia (Essentials and up) include captions.
- **Say what you click.** "Open Settings, then Billing" helps a viewer who cannot see the cursor well and anyone listening on a commute.
- **Make text readable on a phone.** Browser zoom of 110 to 125 percent and slide text you can read at arm's length.
- **Avoid colour-only meaning.** "Click the green button" fails for some viewers; add the label.
- **Publish a text version.** For a converted article you already have it.

## If the lesson is going to YouTube

The same structure works for a YouTube tutorial with three additions. Put chapter timestamps in the description so viewers can jump; Tella generates AI chapters. Make the first frame or thumbnail show the finished result. Keep the opening to the outcome, with no channel intro before it. ### When I would skip Tella for course work

- **You sell into an LMS that expects SCORM.** Use Camtasia.
- **You record on a phone.** Tella has no iOS or Android app, and mobile browsers record camera and microphone only, with no editing. Loom and ScreenPal have mobile apps.
- **Your budget is zero for now.** OBS for recording, or Cap's free plan for personal use.
- **Your lessons are mostly audio with light visuals.** Descript fits that shape better.
