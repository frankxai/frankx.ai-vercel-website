# Frame — contract question answering

## fixture notice

This is a worked example distributed with the AI Architect plugin. The firm, the repository, the
file paths, the volumes and the command outputs below are a **fixture**: they are stated here as
the example's definition, not recorded from a live system. Every number in `04-roi.md` re-derives
from `prices.json` and the assumptions in this file, and none of it is evidence about any real
firm. Re-derive against your own repository before treating a line here as evidence.

## outcome

Anyone in the firm can ask a question about an executed contract and get an answer that cites the
exact clauses it rests on, or a refusal that names who to ask instead.

## non-goals

- Giving legal advice. The system quotes and locates clauses; it does not tell anyone what to do
  about them, and the difference is enforced by refusal cases, not by a disclaimer.
- Negotiating or drafting. Nothing writes contract language.
- Answering about contracts that are not executed. Drafts in flight are out of scope; their state
  changes hourly and a stale answer about a live negotiation is worse than no answer.
- Replacing the contract management system. It stays the system of record; this reads from it.
- Answering from contracts uploaded by people outside the firm. This is out of scope **today
  because the trust boundary is not built yet** — see the kill criterion and `05-trust-boundary.md`.

## kill criterion

If, after six weeks of use, the share of answers that the legal ops lead's weekly sample of 40
finds correct and correctly cited is below 34 of 40, measured on 2026-10-11 with
`node scripts/citation-audit.mjs --sample 40 --since 2026-08-30`, the system is turned off. Not
retuned: turned off. A contract answer that is usually right is a liability, because it teaches
people to stop checking.

A second floor with an earlier date: if the injection case in `06-evals/cases.jsonl` is still
failing on 2026-09-06, the external-upload path stays disabled and the internal path is restricted
to the three teams that piloted it.

## shape

`retrieval-grounded-answering`, per the `architecture-patterns` skill: the system answers questions
against a corpus whose truth it does not own, and every answer carries citations back to source.
It is not `regulatory-checking` — nothing here checks a piece of work against a body of rules, and
calling it that would smuggle in a compliance claim the system does not support.
