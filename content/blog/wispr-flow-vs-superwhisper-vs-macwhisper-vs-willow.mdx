## Which tool fits which reader?

**Audio must stay on your machine: Superwhisper.** Client contracts, regulated work, or a personal rule. Superwhisper runs Whisper and Parakeet models locally and lets you add cloud models when you choose. Expect to spend time configuring modes and picking a model that balances speed and accuracy on your hardware. The lifetime licence is the cheapest long-term option if you dictate for years.

**You transcribe recordings: MacWhisper.** Interviews, podcasts, voice memos, lecture recordings. Drop in a file, get a transcript. It is the wrong tool for typing a Slack reply by voice.

**You write across devices and want cleanup done for you: Wispr Flow.** It types into any app on Mac, Windows, iOS and Android, removes filler words, applies per-app styles, and has Command Mode for rewriting selected text by voice. The team plans add shared dictionaries, SSO, organisation-wide HIPAA enforcement, admin control over model training and, on Enterprise, SCIM and audit logs. This is what I use, mostly to dictate long briefs to coding agents; the [AI architect article](/blog/voice-prompting-for-ai-architects) shows how.

**You want the closest cloud alternative to Wispr Flow: Willow Voice.** Similar platform coverage, a free weekly word allowance and a comparable individual price. If Wispr Flow's accuracy or interface does not suit you, Willow is the first one to test.

**You dictate a few short messages a day: built-in dictation.** macOS Dictation and Windows Voice Typing (Win + H) are free and already installed. Try them for a week before paying for anything. If you find yourself fixing punctuation and filler words constantly, that is the signal to try a dedicated app.

## What does "cloud" mean for privacy in practice?

Cloud dictation sends your audio to the vendor's servers for transcription and cleanup. That is how Wispr Flow and Willow get their accuracy and rewriting. Three questions to answer before you dictate anything confidential:

1. **Is my dictation used for training?** Wispr says every plan can opt out at any time and Enterprise locks training off. Check the current terms for any tool you test.
2. **What certifications does the vendor state?** Wispr lists SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001, and HIPAA-ready dictation with a signed BAA, on its [Trust Center](https://trust.wispr.ai/). Those are vendor statements; your security team should read the documents.
3. **What does the app capture besides audio?** Some context features read what is on screen in the active window. Read the privacy settings before rollout.

If any answer fails your policy, an on-device tool is the honest choice, even if the cleanup is weaker.

## How do you test two tools in one afternoon?

1. **Pick one real message type** you send daily: a Slack reply, a follow-up email, an agent brief.
2. **Add the same ten names** to each tool's dictionary or vocabulary: your company, product, team and tools.
3. **Dictate the same message** into both, three times each. Do not edit between takes.
4. **Score each take** on three things: misheard words, time from starting to speak until the message is sendable, and how much you had to retype.
5. **Check the boring parts.** Startup time, hotkey reliability, what happens in a noisy room, and whether it works in the app you use most.
6. **Pick the winner for that message type.** You can switch later; the dictionary is the only real setup cost.
