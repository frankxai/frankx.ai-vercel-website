The fastest way to choose a text-to-speech provider is to give two of them a script that causes problems in your work. Use the product names your narrator mispronounces, the number formats your customers actually hear, and the one sentence where the delivery has to change. The winner is the tool that gives you a usable take within your budget.

## Which ElevenLabs alternative should you try first?

This is a shortlist by production job, based on provider documentation checked on 24 November 2026. There is no shared listening test behind these recommendations. Use the same material and scorecard before ordering the tools by quality or cost.

| Start with | When it belongs on your shortlist | What to measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Fish Audio](https://fish.audio/plan/) | You generate recurring narration or voiceovers and want another hosted API candidate. | The bill for your actual script, the number of retakes and the edit time required to finish a minute. |
| [Cartesia](https://cartesia.ai/pricing) | Audio must start quickly enough for a live conversation. | Time to first playable audio and end-to-end response latency from the region that serves your users. |
| [Hume](https://www.hume.ai/octave) | Delivery instructions and emotional shifts are central to the recording. | Whether a listener hears the intended change and whether you can reproduce it on the next take. |
| [Kokoro](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M) | You want local generation with preset voices. The weights use Apache 2.0. | Pronunciation, generation speed on your machine and the time it takes to package the workflow. |
| [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api) | Your shortlist needs a hosted benchmark for long narration, localization or an existing Studio workflow. | Voice consistency across a long passage, correction time and the cost of rerendering a section. |

## How to compare the cost of usable audio

Price the test in the units each vendor actually bills. A million input characters, a million bytes, a credit allowance and a minute of generated speech describe different things. UTF-8 text can use several bytes for one character. A credit can have a different value across models. For a subscription, include unused capacity when you compare it with metered API usage.

Use this first-pass test.

1. **Run the same three excerpts.** Use a clean narration sample, a pronunciation stress test and a paragraph with a deliberate change of pace or emotion. For multilingual work, run the test once per required language.
2. **Write down what makes a take usable.** Mark pronunciation, timing, intelligibility and edits. For a live agent, record median and slow-tail response times on the full stack. A provider's preview latency leaves out the rest of your call path.
3. **Keep the rejected takes.** Count their billed usage. Divide total test spend by the minutes you would actually ship, and record editing time separately.
4. **Confirm the voice rights.** Use your own voice or an authorized voice. Check that the license covers your output, market and publishing channel.

An illustrative test might cost $0.72 in API usage and produce four minutes you would ship, or $0.18 per accepted minute. If it took eight minutes to fix pronunciation and trim gaps, log those eight minutes alongside the API bill. That editing time can change the buying decision.

## When should you keep ElevenLabs?

Keep it in the trial when you need consistent long-form delivery, when your required languages sound good on your actual script, or when your team already works in Studio and can take a draft to a finished export with fewer handoffs. A short social clip, a live phone interaction and an audiobook each need their own acceptance criteria.
