**TL;DR**: Still → motion → audio → edit → QA. That is the cinematic factory. Do not generate random video first. Lock keyframes, brand, and shot list.

## Why stills first

Random text-to-video wastes budget. Keyframes fix:

- character/brand consistency  
- composition  
- palette  
- text-critical frames  

Then motion inherits discipline.

## Pipeline steps

1. **Narrative + shot list** (3–7 shots)  
2. **Image generation** with brand-locked prompts (Spartan Sage, palette)  
3. **Image-to-video** per shot (route model by style/realism)  
4. **Audio** (VO via ElevenLabs-class tools; music OS for beds)  
5. **Edit + captions** (Descript / CapCut / Remotion)  
6. **Brand + claims QA**  
7. **Distribute** (blog hero loop, Reels, X, landing)

## Timing budgets

| Asset | Length | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| Social loop | 6–12s | one idea, one CTA |
| Explainer | 15–30s | architecture / product |
| Launch film | 45–90s | multi-shot story |

## Cost control

- Draft motion on cheaper models  
- Final on premium  
- Cap seconds per post  
- Reuse motion templates and camera moves  

## Fitness queen / founder use cases

- Founder: architecture explainers, product teardowns  
- Fitness queen: cinematic workout openers, owned-audience landing loops (not rented-platform only)

## AEO: How do I build an AI image-to-video pipeline?

Start with a shot list and brand-locked stills, animate shot-by-shot with the best model for style or realism, add audio, then edit and run a brand QA gate before publish.
