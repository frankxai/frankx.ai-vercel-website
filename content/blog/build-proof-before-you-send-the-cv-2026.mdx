**TL;DR:** The CV pile got bigger and more uniform this year. [Workday's October 2026 report](https://newsroom.workday.com/2026-10-05-Workday-Global-Workforce-Report-AI-Is-Rewriting-Jobs-More-Than-Its-Cutting-Them) counts a median 69 applicants per filled job, up from 58, with 84% of job seekers using AI in their search. A polished document now proves very little. A small working project, explained on your own page and in a short video, gives a reviewer something they can check. The same project can also become your first paid offer.

If you are job hunting right now and watching the economic news with a knot in your stomach, this piece is for you. I am writing it because I think many people in that position are underrating one thing they control: the ability to show finished work before anyone gives them a title.

I am not promising that hiring got easy. It did not. I am saying the cost of building a credible demonstration has dropped, while the value of an unverified CV has dropped faster. That gap is the opening.

## What changed in the hiring funnel this year?

Three numbers explain the mood.

| Signal | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applications per job, North America | 116 in 2022, 244 in 2025 | [Greenhouse, The Hire Standard](https://www.greenhouse.com/recruiting-benchmarks) |
| Median applicants per filled job | 58 to 69, year over year | [Workday, October 2026](https://newsroom.workday.com/2026-10-05-Workday-Global-Workforce-Report-AI-Is-Rewriting-Jobs-More-Than-Its-Cutting-Them) |
| Hiring managers who say AI-enhanced CVs make skills harder to verify | 65% | [Robert Half, March 2026](https://press.roberthalf.com/2026-03-10-Robert-Half-survey-67-of-HR-leaders-report-AI-generated-applications-are-slowing-hiring) |

Read those together. More people apply, more of the documents were written with the same tools, and the people reading them trust the documents less. Workday also reports that time to fill held at about 60 days, and that employers say roles mostly stay open because candidates lack the right skills.

The pile is full. The shortlist still has empty seats.

## Which skills are employers actually paying for?

Workday tracked a shift that matters for anyone learning AI right now. Demand for basic AI skills such as simple prompting peaked in January 2026 and then fell 25%. Demand for hands-on skills such as building AI tools, automating workflows and AI engineering climbed 51% between September 2025 and July 2026 ([Workday](https://newsroom.workday.com/2026-10-05-Workday-Global-Workforce-Report-AI-Is-Rewriting-Jobs-More-Than-Its-Cutting-Them)).

Writing "proficient in ChatGPT" on a CV now signals the skill that is losing value. Showing a workflow you built, tested and documented signals the one that is gaining.

The longer view points the same way. Employers in the [World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/3-skills-outlook) expect 39% of workers' core skills to change by 2030. AI and big data top the fastest-growing list. Analytical thinking is still the most-cited core skill, and creative thinking, resilience and curiosity are rising too. Those are employer expectations, and they reward people who can learn in public and judge what they build.

[LinkedIn's Future of Recruiting 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-acquisition/future-of-recruiting-2025) adds the recruiter side: 93% of talent professionals say accurately assessing skills is crucial to quality of hire. Make that assessment easy for them.

## Can a newcomer really outperform people with years of experience?

At everything, no. At one specific, current problem, sometimes yes.

Experience carries judgment, relationships and memory of what breaks in production. Tools do not hand those over. In [METR's randomized trial](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/), 16 experienced open-source developers took 19% longer on real tasks when AI tools were allowed, while believing they had been faster. METR's [February 2026 update](https://metr.substack.com/p/2026-02-24-uplift-update) says speedups now look likely, but selection effects make the size hard to measure. Access to a tool and measured performance are different things, for veterans and newcomers alike.

So the credible ambition is narrow: become demonstrably good at one outcome the team needs this quarter. A person with ten years in a field may know the field deeply and still not have rebuilt their workflow around current tools. A person with two years may have done exactly that for one task. Your edge is that one task, shown working.

Some concrete shapes:

- **Marketing:** a researched campaign for one customer segment, with draft creative, a channel plan and the test you would run first.
- **Operations:** a prototype that turns messy inbound requests into structured records, with a human check for uncertain cases.
- **Customer success:** a rebuilt onboarding sequence that handles the five most common points of confusion.
- **Engineering:** a small shipped app with tests, a README and a written list of known limits.
- **Sales:** a researched target account, a plausible problem hypothesis and a three-touch outreach sequence.

Each one moves the conversation from "I am motivated" to "this is how I work."

## Understand the audience, then the tool

Knowing the newest model is a weak edge. It expires every quarter.

A stronger edge is knowing how a specific audience finds information, decides what to trust and what format they actually finish. That includes how younger buyers and colleagues use short video, search, communities and AI answers. "I understand Gen Z" means nothing on its own. Turn it into evidence:

- Who exactly is the audience, and what are they trying to get done?
- Where do they look for answers today?
- Which formats help them understand or trust something?
- What did you observe beyond your own habits?
- What would you test before spending money?

Five real conversations and one clearly described experiment beat a confident deck of generational stereotypes. And I would not build an identity around one model or platform. The durable skill is investigating, building, evaluating and explaining. The interface will change again by spring.

## What should the website show?

A portfolio can look beautiful and still answer the wrong question. "Everything I have ever made" makes the reviewer do the work. "Why I can help with this role" does the work for them.

Build a simple site with two or three substantial projects. Then, for the few roles you care most about, add one page tailored to that role.

### Lead with the problem

Weak: *I am a passionate, AI-driven growth professional.*

Specific: *I rebuilt the first-week onboarding for a subscription app so new users reach their first useful result in one session.*

The second line gives the reader something to inspect.

### Put the deliverable on the page

A live demo, an interactive prototype, the campaign with its research, a recorded workflow run, a dashboard with clearly labelled sample data, or a repository with setup steps and tests. Do not make someone book a call to find out whether there is substance behind the headline.

### Show your judgment

Say what you chose not to automate, what you checked, where the data came from and how the thing fails. If AI helped, say which parts it did and which parts you did. A hiring source quoted by [Deel](https://www.deel.com/deel-works/ai-resume-crisis/) put it plainly: a polished portfolio link means less now, because tools generate clean mockups that never get built. Your decisions and your failure notes are the part a tool cannot fake for you.

### Separate results from assumptions

"This increased conversion by 30%" and "this is a proposed redesign I have not tested with real users; here is the experiment I would run" are very different claims. Never invent customers, testimonials, revenue or metrics. A speculative project still shows excellent thinking when its limits are written down.

### Connect it to the role

End each case study with the responsibilities it demonstrates and what you would still need to learn. That paragraph is where the reviewer decides to call you.

## Why add a video?

Because it lets a busy person watch you think in 90 seconds. It will not guarantee attention, and not every role needs you on camera. A screen recording with your voice is enough.

A structure that works:

1. The problem I looked at.
2. What I built.
3. The most important decision I made.
4. What works, what is untested, what I would do next.
5. How this maps to your role.

Add captions or a transcript, and make sure the page works without the video. Keep it short, specific and unpolished enough to sound like you. When your application sits next to a stack of similar CVs, a reviewer who has seen you explain real work remembers you.

## Does tailoring still pay?

Volume alone does not. In [Huntr's Q3 2026 data](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q3-2026), users who sent 11 to 20 applications in the quarter had an 8.0% interview rate, against 2.4% for those who sent more than 100. When the same people tripled their usual weekly volume, their interview rate fell from 9.5% to 5.6%. More applications did produce more total interviews, so the trade-off is real. Huntr also measured a median 102 days to a first offer in Q3 2026. Plan for a search measured in months.

My read: send a focused number of applications backed by one strong project, and spend the saved hours on the tailored page and the video.

## How does the same project become a side business?

This is the part job seekers overlook. One project can serve several routes at once.

Say you build an enquiry-triage prototype for small clinics. You can use it as:

- evidence of your operations and technical skills in applications;
- a reason to start conversations with teams that have that problem;
- the base of a small, fixed-scope implementation offer;
- a cheap test of whether anyone cares enough to pay.

It becomes a business when someone pays for the outcome and you can deliver it reliably at a cost that works. Start smaller than "launch an AI agency": one customer type, one recurring problem, one bounded offer. Talk to five potential buyers before you build much more. Then decide whether you want to keep delivering it.

Respect your employment agreements, confidentiality and data rules. Never put a current or former employer's internal data into a public demo or an unapproved AI service.

## Where is the line on unpaid work?

Do not build bespoke systems for every company that might reject you. Build one strong core project for the kind of role you want, then tailor the framing and examples for a handful of serious targets. Use public information, label your assumptions and set a time box.

If an employer asks for substantial production-ready work, ask about scope, payment and ownership first. And still submit the CV when the process asks for it. The portfolio adds a route to credibility; it does not replace the application instructions.

## A 30-day plan

1. Read 15 to 20 postings for the role you want and list the outcomes that repeat.
2. Pick one problem you can work on with public information only.
3. Build a small, complete version in two weeks.
4. Test it, write down where it breaks, fix the worst part.
5. Publish the case study on your own domain.
6. Record a 90-second walkthrough with captions.
7. Send a focused set of applications that link the project to each role.
8. Use the replies, or the silence, to decide: go deeper, change direction, or test a paid version.

The goal is one credible piece of evidence. A perfect personal brand can wait.

## Be honest about the limits

Not everyone struggling to find work has missed an opportunity. People carry different financial pressure, caregiving, health and time constraints. A portfolio cannot end a hiring freeze, remove discrimination or guarantee a reply.

Still, waiting for permission and making your ability visible are two very different strategies. You do not need to convince the market you are ahead of everyone. You need the right person to see that you understand a real problem and can do something useful about it.

Your CV says where you have been. What you build shows where you are ready to go.

## FAQ

### Do I need to code to build a portfolio project in 2026?

No. Many strong projects are a researched campaign, a redesigned process, a documented workflow built with no-code tools, or an analysis with clear sources. What matters is that the reviewer can inspect a finished artifact and your reasoning.

### Should I mention that I used AI to build my project?

Yes. Say which parts AI did and which decisions you made, checked and corrected. Hiding it creates risk; describing it shows exactly the hands-on skill employers report wanting more of.

### How long should a portfolio walkthrough video be?

Aim for 60 to 120 seconds. Cover the problem, what you built, one key decision, what is untested and how it connects to the role. Add captions or a transcript.

### Is it worth building a side business while job hunting?

It can be, if it is small and bounded. One customer type, one problem and one fixed-scope offer let you test demand while the same work strengthens your applications. Check your employment agreements first.
