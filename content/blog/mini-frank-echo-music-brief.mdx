Mini Frank is sitting on piano keys alongside Echo. Imagine handing that picture to a drummer. There’s a lovely idea on the page, and the drummer still needs to know when to come in.

[Open the first studio sheet at full size](/images/guides/mini-me/collection-01-01.svg). Its entire premise fits in a breath. A feeling. Then a song. Keep that sheet in view while you read the second one.

## What the second sheet gives the musicians

![Mini Frank and Echo’s illustrated music brief, with the mood, tempo, percussion and vocal direction](/images/guides/mini-me/collection-02-08.svg)

The second sheet calls for 139 BPM house, Afro percussion, a breathy German and English lead, and a close verse that opens into a wide chorus. Its most useful instruction is the order of two feelings. Restless comes first. Released comes second. Without that order, the same instruments could play for four minutes and the song could finish exactly where it started.

The tempo gives the drummer a clock. It leaves the interesting question open. Where does the listener feel the release? One way to answer is to keep the kick steady and let the voice do it. Start with one close vocal in the verse. Save the first doubled line for the chorus. Another is to keep the singer close throughout and let the percussion answer the last word. Those are two arrangements you can audition from the same drawing.

## Give one person one decision

Write two feelings on a card. Pick the bar where the second one arrives. Hand the card to a friend and give them control of one instrument. As an example, try homesick into playful, bar nine, piano. Let them decide what the left hand does when the feeling changes.

Record eight bars on either side of that moment. The smallest useful test is to play the recording to somebody who hasn’t seen the card. Ask them where they heard a change. They might pick bar seven, where the piano first gives the game away. That answer is worth another take.
