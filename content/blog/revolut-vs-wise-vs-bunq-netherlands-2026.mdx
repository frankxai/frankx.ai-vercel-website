## Choose Revolut when the app breadth matters

Revolut combines a Dutch euro account, multi-currency balances, card controls, virtual cards, travel features, savings, investing, Revolut Pro, and a five-plan ladder in one app. Standard has no monthly subscription, although card delivery and usage fees can apply.

The strongest Revolut use case is not a single cheap transfer. It is a repeated cross-border workflow: hold currencies, pay by card, freeze or replace virtual card details, manage travel spending, and upgrade only if the plan economics work.

The trade-off is complexity. Exchange allowances, weekend fees, partner products, insurance, subscriptions, and plan benefits create more terms to understand. If you only need to convert and send one currency, the extra surface area may not help.

Read the [current Revolut fees that matter](/blog/revolut-fees-netherlands-2026) before choosing it for travel.

## Choose Wise when the transfer quote is the product

Wise's model is easier to express: show a route-specific conversion fee and use the mid-market rate. Its Netherlands pricing page currently says conversion fees vary by currency and start from 0.2%. A physical debit card currently costs €7 with no subscription.

For Europe, Wise currently lists €250 a month of ATM withdrawals before a 2.69% variable fee on the amount above the allowance. ATM-owner fees can still apply.

Wise Europe SA is authorised as a payment institution. Wise says it safeguards customer funds by keeping them separate from its own money. Safeguarding is not the same legal mechanism as a bank deposit guarantee. If that distinction is decisive for a large balance, read Wise's EEA safeguarding explanation and decide what role the account should play.

Wise is the cleanest comparison candidate for:

- a large or recurring international transfer;
- getting paid with multi-currency account details;
- a traveller who prefers pay-per-conversion pricing over plan bundles;
- a user who wants the final recipient amount front and centre.

## Choose bunq when Dutch banking workflow matters

bunq is a Dutch-licensed bank. Its current personal plans are Free at €0, Core at €3.99/month, Pro at €9.99/month, and Elite at €18.99/month.

The differentiator is account structure. Core currently includes up to five bank accounts, while Pro and Elite include up to 25, each with its own IBAN. That makes bunq useful for envelope-style budgeting, tax pots, household separation, or a more complete primary-bank setup.

bunq says eligible euro deposits are covered by the Dutch Deposit Guarantee up to €100,000 per depositor. It also offers the Dutch bank switch service for moving incoming payments and direct debits.

For foreign-currency card payments, bunq's current help content lists a 0.5% network fee when the card payment is in a different currency. Other transfer, SWIFT, account, and plan limits need a live pricing-sheet check.

bunq is the strongest candidate when your primary job is structured Dutch daily banking rather than the lowest one-off conversion quote.

## Which is cheapest for travel?

There is no stable answer without an amount and behaviour pattern.

- A Revolut Standard user staying within the weekday exchange and ATM allowances can pay no Revolut exchange fee, but weekend, fair-usage, delivery, and ATM rules matter.
- A Wise user pays the route-specific conversion fee and can compare the recipient amount directly; the current minimum headline starts at 0.2%, not zero.
- A bunq user pays for the chosen account plan if not on Free and should include the foreign-currency network fee and any relevant plan limits.

Compare three scenarios using each provider's live screen:

1. €1,000 card spending across a trip;
2. one €1,000 cash-equivalent exchange and transfer;
3. two €250 ATM withdrawals.

Run the quotes at the same time. Currency markets move, and a comparison made hours apart is weak evidence.

## Which is best as a primary bank?

For a Dutch resident prioritising salary, direct debits, bank switching, multiple current accounts, and Dutch scheme administration, bunq has the clearest primary-bank orientation.

Revolut now offers a Dutch IBAN and direct-debit/salary functionality and may be entirely workable as a primary account. I still prefer a two-provider resilience model for travel and mobile-first finance: one account for continuity, one specialist account for cross-border use.

Wise is better treated as a money-movement and multi-currency account than as a direct substitute for every traditional bank function. Its safeguarding structure and product focus are different.

## Which is safer?

All three are regulated, but the legal protection model differs:

- Revolut Bank eligible Netherlands-branch deposits: Lithuanian deposit guarantee, subject to terms;
- bunq eligible euro deposits: Dutch Deposit Guarantee, subject to terms;
- Wise cash balances: safeguarding under the Wise Europe payment-institution framework, not ordinary bank deposit insurance.

“Regulated” does not make an authorised scam payment, investment loss, account-access issue, or every product balance reimbursable. Read the [regulator-first Revolut safety guide](/blog/is-revolut-safe-netherlands-2026) for the distinctions.

## My decision tree

Choose **Revolut** if you want one broad app for travel, card controls, currencies, and optional plan benefits.

Choose **Wise** if the primary job is converting, sending, or receiving money internationally with a clear per-route quote.

Choose **bunq** if the primary job is Dutch daily banking with multiple IBAN accounts and structured budgeting.

Choose **two providers** if losing access to one app during travel or a critical payment would create a serious problem.

The last option is often the mature one. Redundancy is a feature.

## FAQ

### Is Revolut better than Wise in the Netherlands?

Revolut is broader and can be better for travel, card controls, and an all-in-one app. Wise can be better for route-specific international transfers and transparent pay-per-conversion pricing. Compare the live final amount for your actual transaction.

### Is Revolut better than bunq?

Revolut is stronger as a multi-currency travel and financial super-app. bunq is stronger for a Dutch primary-bank workflow, multiple IBAN accounts, and the Dutch Deposit Guarantee. The right answer depends on the job.

### Which has a Dutch IBAN?

Revolut advertises a Dutch IBAN for its Netherlands euro account, and bunq issues Dutch IBAN accounts. Wise provides EUR account details but operates as a payment institution rather than a Dutch bank.

### Which is cheapest for currency exchange?

It depends on currency, amount, timing, plan, and funding route. Revolut can have no additional weekday exchange fee within a plan allowance; Wise charges a route-specific fee currently advertised from 0.2%; bunq's foreign-currency rules depend on the account and transaction. Compare live quotes.

### Should I keep more than one travel card?

Yes if payment continuity matters. Independent providers reduce the impact of a lost card, app outage, merchant rejection, account review, or ATM network issue.
