One version of *Star Show Us* gives you dust on somebody’s shoes before it asks you to follow a star. The other goes straight to the invitation. Same title, same chorus. A different way to meet the listener.

Both recordings are in the FrankX catalog. The longer one is the release featured on the homepage. Grab your headphones and give each opening thirty seconds.

## Two openings to play

[Play the 4:26 version on Suno](https://suno.com/song/e7d082d3-8ecd-4fdb-a8fa-582026554153). The lyric sheet begins with a verse. There’s a hand on a chest, an old drum, a pair of dusty shoes. By the time the chorus looks up at the sky, somebody has already walked a little way to get there.

[Play the 4:09 version on Suno](https://suno.com/song/89eefa0c-d5f7-4bff-8700-015f672917ee). This lyric sheet opens at the pre-chorus. The invitation to find your way back arrives before you have a character in mind. Listen for which part you remember when you pause it. A line of words? The shape of the vocal? The violin?

The musical brief for both versions calls for Latin progressive house, jazz piano and a violin hook over a steady four-on-the-floor beat. Those are the instructions the recordings share. Two generated performances can also differ in phrasing, timing and arrangement, so you can hear more than the lyric edit at work here.

## What the verse buys you

The longer opening gives the chorus a person to belong to. That matters with a line as broad as looking to the stars for direction. A listener might forget an instruction to find their purpose. They can still picture the shoes.

The shorter opening leaves room for a different pleasure. Someone hearing it for the first time can join the refrain without learning a story first. Play it for a friend who has never heard either version. Ask what stayed with them before you tell them which one you prefer.

## Try it on an unfinished track

Take one chorus and keep the vocal take, tempo and backing fixed in your editor. Export one opening with four lines of scene-setting and another that goes straight to the lift. Play both at the same volume, with the filenames hidden. When the hook arrives, write down what it means to you in each version.

That is a more controlled comparison than generating two new tracks. It gives you a decision you can use in the next session: whether this particular chorus needs a story before it, or whether the room is already ready to sing.
