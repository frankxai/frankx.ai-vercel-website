A faceless video still needs an author. Choose one question you can answer with evidence, write the argument, then use ElevenLabs for narration and Higgsfield for the shots the explanation needs.

The workflow below builds a 60–90-second explainer. Its timings are editorial targets. It contains original planning templates and links to official product documentation; it makes no claim about guaranteed audience growth or revenue.

## Start with a question you can demonstrate

Example: **Why does an AI video face change between shots?** The explanation can show an approved reference, a rejected frame and a corrected shot. Those artifacts give the viewer something to inspect.

Avoid a script consisting of unsourced facts over unrelated cinematic footage. Write each claim beside its evidence source. Where your evidence is a simulation, illustration or vendor demonstration, label it accordingly.

| Beat | Editorial target | Visual job |
|---|---|---|
| Open | 0–8 seconds | Show the visible problem |
| Explain | 8–30 seconds | Compare reference and output |
| Demonstrate | 30–65 seconds | Show the correction and its limits |
| Close | 65–90 seconds | Give one useful next action |

These are proposed timing ranges. Measure the approved narration before generating visuals.

## Produce and approve the voice first

Draft the script in short spoken sentences. Read it aloud. Remove a sentence when it only repeats the preceding sentence in more ambitious language.

In [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io), select a voice you are authorized to use. Check the current [voice and speech documentation](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview) and your plan's usage terms. Generate a short sample containing the difficult words: Higgsfield, a model name, a technical acronym or a person's name.

Review pronunciation, pauses and delivery. Correct the script or pronunciation settings before generating the complete track. Save the approved audio file and script version together. Use a cloned voice only with the necessary rights and consent.

## Build a visual plan against the audio

```text
Time range:
Spoken sentence:
Evidence source:
Visual type: screen recording / diagram / real footage / generated B-roll
Reference asset:
Required action:
Camera intention:
Acceptance rule:
Rights and disclosure notes:
```

Screen recordings and diagrams usually explain software behavior more directly than generated footage. Use Higgsfield for an establishing scene, a metaphor clearly labeled as an illustration, or a continuity-sensitive presenter sequence. Keep text, code and meaningful interface details in real captures.

## Generate only the missing shots

For a studio cutaway, use an approved reference and one action:

```text
Animate the approved desk reference into a brief quiet cutaway.
The notebook stays closed. Window light stays constant.
A small movement of the curtain is the only scene action.
Camera holds a stable medium-wide view; no cut or new object.
Leave a clean beginning and end for an editor.
```

This is a proposed shot brief. Check your selected model's supported duration and aspect ratio before submitting. Review object permanence and motion after generation.

When the narration refers to a specific product feature, show the actual feature. A plausible generated dashboard is not evidence of a working interface.

## Assemble the video around the explanation

Place the approved narration on the timeline first. Mark the argument's turns. Add only the visuals needed to support them, then trim B-roll to the useful action rather than using every generated second.

Keep the voice intelligible against music and effects. Review on ordinary phone speakers as well as headphones. Read captions at normal playback speed; correct names, punctuation and line breaks. Inspect where YouTube Shorts or Instagram controls cover the screen before creating a vertical cut.

Retain a clean master, an audio-only export, a caption file and the project file. Create platform variants from that master. Reframing a landscape shot requires a new composition check; a centered crop can remove the evidence the scene depends on.

## Measure the economics before subscribing for volume

Track narration cost, generation debits, rejected attempts, editing time and accepted output. Divide total generation credits by the number of usable clips. That is more useful than comparing the cheapest advertised render.

The [Higgsfield credit guide](/guides/higgsfield-credits-pricing-guide) supplies a small worked example. Use your actual account prices and costs. Subscription comparisons belong after you have measured one representative episode.

## Publication checklist

- Every factual claim has an inspectable source or your own visible demonstration.
- Synthetic scenes cannot be mistaken for documentary evidence.
- Voice, music, footage and image rights cover the intended use.
- Captions match the approved narration and remain readable on a phone.
- The title names the question answered; the first scene actually answers it.
- The description links to the written companion and identifies relevant affiliate relationships.
- The exported master plays correctly before upload.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need generated footage for every sentence?

No. Use the visual that explains the sentence: a screen recording, diagram, real object or generated illustration. Reusing good references and stills can also keep the visual plan within budget.

### Should I generate narration and video simultaneously?

For a narration-led explainer, approve the voice first. Its actual duration determines the visual plan and prevents expensive clips that do not fit the edit.

### Does faceless YouTube guarantee monetization?

No. Originality, audience response, rights and platform policy remain separate requirements. This production method makes no income or monetization guarantee.
