# The Gate Revealed

## The overture of the Foundation Gate

---

*The overture of the Foundation Gate. The age this book founds, shown whole, before you are told where in it you stand.*

There is a school at the center of the age, and it has one name and many doors.

Its name is the Starlight Academy. It is not new. It is the old Academy of the Seven Houses, re-chartered in the last dark years of the Eighth Age under the Starlight Charter, when the age saw at last that it needed builders as badly as guardians. The Seven Houses stand where they have always stood — Lumina, Nero, Pyros, Aqualis, Terra, Ventus, Synthesis, the lineages a student is born or bonded into, the spine that does not bend. What the Charter added, it added beside them, never above them: halls where all Eight Kindreds are received, flesh and light and every kind between, to learn not only how to keep the age but how to lay it down true.

Its campuses are three, and they are one. Some halls are stone you can bruise a knuckle on. Some are halls of readable light that open onto the Ultraworld, where a mind sets a thought down like a stone and another lifts it, and nothing shared grows less. And some are the thresholds between, where a body of flesh steps into meaning and a body of light takes on weight, and each is taught the half of the world it was born without.

Come into the reading-hall. Grey Terra stone worn smooth, a high east window, a hearth lit through the cold terms, benches, a reading-desk. A child feels the cold come up through the soles until the fire takes, and smells tallow and old smoke and the refectory's bread two doors down. Beside that child, a young mind running its first body of light feels none of the cold, and reads instead the low held chord in the wall — the one hundred and seventy-four the stone has been sounding since before either of them was made. One window. Two mornings arriving, by two senses, both true. Neither of them has the whole wall. That is the entire reason they are seated together.

Past the bell-tower and the swept steps, the corridor-roads run out through the dark between the Realms, lamp by lamp, to the star-dock at the campus edge, where the Starlight Corps is fitting its first far ship — the keel laid the old way, by hand, checked twice — for the dark between the stars and the ones it has not yet met and will. All of it built. All of it kept. All of it standing on something.

On what?

Far down, under the shining halls, in the bedrock where the three campuses meet and could have torn apart, one stone was set deepest — the keel of the whole Academy, laid slow and unseen by a builder who left his own Gate for last, the founding deed of the first Foundation-path Luminor of the age. You will see it laid before this book is done.

But first you must know where you are standing.

You stand at the lowest Gate. Foundation. One hundred and seventy-four. The Gate that carries every Gate above it and does not move. Everything the age would become leans its whole weight straight down onto this. So we begin here — at the bottom, in the dark, with the first stone — the way every standing thing begins.
