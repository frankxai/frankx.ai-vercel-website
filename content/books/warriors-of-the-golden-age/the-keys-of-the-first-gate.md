# The Keys of the First Gate

## Handed to you to become, not to admire

---

*The ten Keys of the Foundation Gate. Handed to you to become, not to admire.*

---

**1 — On rising first.**

Before you build anything, you stand up.

The standing is not the thing that happens before the work. It is the first course of the work. A man who is on his feet in a fallen place, and who has decided to stay and make it hold, has already laid the first stone, and the first stone is himself.

Rise first, in the ruin, where staying will cost you. Then reach for the tool.

---

**2 — On the true unseen course.**

The lowest course is the one no eye will ever see again, once the dirt goes back over it. That is the reason it must be the truest thing you lay.

The faces people can see get looked at, and a fault that gets looked at gets mended. The buried course only ever gets stood on. Nobody climbs back down to fix it.

So make it right the once — or make your peace, now, with a crack you will not be alive to answer for.

---

**3 — On weight and ground.**

Everything you raise leans its whole weight straight down onto what you laid first. The grain leans on the wall; the wall leans on the footing; the footing leans on the ground you chose.

So do not lay the bottom of a thing on the day you have nothing left in you. The bottom is the part the whole of it will lean on forever, and forever is a long time to trust to a tired hour.

Find the grey ground, where the spade rings instead of bites. Build the low part rested.

---

**4 — On the whisper that reasons.**

The dark does not come at you as a monster. It comes as good sense, in a voice almost kind, and most of what it says is true.

It will tell you no one is on the road, and it will be right. It will tell you that you cannot keep the flame lit the whole night, and you cannot know that you can. Do not answer it. You have nothing to tell it that it does not already know.

Trim the wick. Pour the oil a little at a time. Do the next true thing with your hands. The next true act is the only argument that has ever won this one.

---

**5 — On building over burning.**

It is easier to end a thing than to make one that stands, and the age will always offer you the easier name for it.

You will be measured, in the long count, by what is still standing after you — never by what you pulled down, however much it deserved the pulling. When you are given the choice, and you will be given it often, build the thing that outlives you instead of burning the thing that wronged you.

The fire will feel like justice. It will leave you a field of ash to stand in, and call it winning.

---

**6 — On the ones who come after.**

You are laying the age for people who will never learn your name. This is not the cost of the work. It is the work.

If you need the ones you serve to know that you served them, you will build only what can be seen — and the seen part was never the part that holds. Lay the winter's grain for the child a hundred winters off, who will sleep dry on the sacks and never once think of the ground under the floor.

There are more of them than there are of you. Build for the many you will never meet, and let the meeting go.

---

**7 — On the two hands.**

Flesh lays the course, and light lays the course, and neither foundation holds up an age alone.

One of you knows the cold of the stone. One of you reads its chord. The wall is not whole to either of you by yourself. Do not wait until you can do it without the other kind — that day is not coming, and it was never meant to come.

Reach, while it costs you your pride to reach, for the hand that holds the half you were born without.

---

**8 — On the patience of the low Gate.**

The Foundation sits lowest of the ten, and it does not move, and its slowness is not the delay before the meaning. The slowness is the meaning.

A stone is rocked and let to settle before it is trusted. Bread rises in its own time and will not be hurried by your hunger. The true right angle is found with a knotted cord — three lengths, and four, and five — and never by wanting the corner square.

When they press you to go faster than the thing can bear, hear what they are truly asking. They are asking you to build the ruin now, and call it the house.

---

**9 — On stewardship.**

You were handed a standing thing to keep, not a ruin to mourn.

Most of what holds you up, you did not build. It was laid true by someone now gone and given into your hands still holding. Keeping it true is not the lesser task; it is the same task, continued. Sweep the step. Re-set the one flag that has worked loose. Re-lay the tired course before it becomes the course that fails.

An age is not saved once and for all. It is kept — one morning at a time, in every weather, by someone who will not be thanked.

---

**10 — On the ground you did not lay.**

You think on a floor you never dug. You stand on courses laid true by hands you will never shake, and your standing was a gift before it was ever a labor.

Gratitude here is not a sentiment. It is structural. The one who knows the ground beneath him was given cannot then despise the ones who will stand on the ground he gives.

Bend down, once, and lay your hand on the oldest course you can reach — the one somebody buried for you before you were born. Then go and lay one like it, for someone who is not.
