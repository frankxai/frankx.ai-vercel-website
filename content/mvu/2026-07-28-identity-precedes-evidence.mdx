## Source boundary

This entry combines several threads from the week: inherited ideas about deservingness, compulsory sacrifice, pressure, judgment, difficulty relaxing, future-self visualization, and the need to rehearse recovery as well as success.

Because the ideas came through multiple sessions and later reflection, I am not assigning individual sentences to a speaker. The model below is mine.

## The sentence

> Identity precedes evidence. Evidence stabilizes identity.

The first half explains why a person can move before the world has confirmed the move.

The second half prevents the idea from becoming fantasy.

A chosen identity can change what I notice, permit, practise, and attempt. But if reality never answers—through behaviour, relationships, customers, craft, or completed work—the identity remains an internal claim.

Identity can open the door. Evidence is what lets the new identity stay.

## What the wealth blueprint exposed

The notes surfaced several rules that can quietly govern a life:

- I must sacrifice to deserve.
- Ease is suspicious.
- Pressure proves commitment.
- Rest must be earned after completion.
- Judgment is safer than being judged.
- Receiving creates a debt I must immediately repay.
- If I relax, the whole system may stop.

These rules do not need to be consciously believed to shape decisions. They appear in the calendar, the offer, the price, the inability to finish, the reflex to add more work, and the difficulty of allowing a good moment to remain simply good.

A blueprint is useful only if it can be observed.

Instead of asking whether I “have” a scarcity identity, I can ask:

- What do I do when support arrives?
- What happens after praise?
- What level of effort feels morally safe?
- Which opportunities become suspicious because they are too direct?
- Where do I add complexity after the path has become clear?
- Can I rest before every task is complete?

Behaviour gives the belief a visible edge.

## Visualization without escape

Visualization can become a beautiful place to avoid the process.

A complete rehearsal needs more than the ideal frame. It needs to include:

- the sensory experience of the desired future;
- the ordinary actions that produce it;
- the interruption that threatens it;
- the moment of doubt or embarrassment;
- the recovery;
- the next small action;
- the evidence that arrives afterward.

If I rehearse only applause, I train attachment to a scene. If I rehearse process and recovery, I train continuity.

The operating model is:

**Chosen identity → sensory rehearsal → process rehearsal → recovery rehearsal → smallest real action → evidence → updated self-concept**

The “recovery rehearsal” is the hinge. A future identity becomes resilient when it knows what to do after the plan fails, the launch is quiet, the message receives no reply, or the old pattern returns.

## Identity as a hypothesis

A grounded identity statement is not a verdict. It is a hypothesis about how I intend to meet reality.

“I am a creator” means I create.

“I am an architect” means I make relationships and constraints visible enough to design.

“I am a trusted guide” means people can trace what I say, where it came from, and what changed because of it.

“I receive well” means support can enter without immediately becoming shame, performance, or another obligation.

The identity earns stability through repeated evidence.

## The evidence ladder

Evidence does not have to begin with a life-changing result.

A useful ladder is:

1. **State evidence** — I can access the posture, language, or attention of the chosen identity.
2. **Action evidence** — I take one behaviour the identity would take.
3. **Craft evidence** — the behaviour produces a clearer or stronger artifact.
4. **Relational evidence** — another person can understand, use, trust, or respond to it.
5. **Market evidence** — somebody allocates attention, reputation, or money.
6. **System evidence** — the result can repeat without depending entirely on a peak state.

Each rung answers the identity with a different kind of reality.

## The smallest real action

After any future-self practice, I need one action small enough to happen now and real enough to generate evidence.

Examples:

- publish the dated field note instead of planning the complete archive;
- ask one participant what changed instead of writing the whole curriculum;
- offer one paid sprint instead of designing the entire academy;
- finish one canonical repository instead of opening five;
- state one boundary instead of imagining a fully sovereign life;
- receive one compliment without deflecting or converting it into work.

The action should be reversible enough to reduce theatre and consequential enough to teach me something.

## My own Week One evidence

At 04:16, after the first week, I recorded:

> “I am amazed by myself and deeply grateful for myself.”

> “Usually I would hold myself small. Tonight I do not want to.”

Those lines matter because they are not abstract affirmations. They came after a week of attending, meeting people, articulating ideas, seeing how I show up, and recognizing work already built.

The identity was not invented that night. It was acknowledged.

The next responsibility is to make the acknowledgment answerable to action.

## A seven-day evidence practice

For one chosen identity:

1. Write the identity as a behaviour, not an adjective.
2. Rehearse the desired moment with sensory detail.
3. Rehearse the boring middle.
4. Rehearse one disruption and recovery.
5. Take the smallest real action within 24 hours.
6. Record what reality returned.
7. Update the identity from evidence instead of protecting it from evidence.

This makes manifestation less about controlling outcomes and more about becoming available for aligned behaviour, useful feedback, and repair.

## What I am carrying forward

I do not need evidence before I am allowed to begin.

I do need evidence if I want the beginning to become a life.

Identity gives direction. Rehearsal expands available action. Recovery keeps the action alive after disruption. Evidence turns a private claim into something the nervous system, the craft, and the world can trust.
